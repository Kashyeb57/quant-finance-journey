Partitioning of Variance in ANOVA

This is where ANOVA finally gets computed. We've defined it (comparing the means of two or more groups), listed its assumptions, and named its three types. Now we perform the hypothesis test step by step — a process also called partitioning the variance, because the whole trick is splitting the total variation into a between-groups piece and a within-groups piece and comparing them.

The hypotheses​

ANOVA compares the means of two or more groups, so the null hypothesis says every group mean is the same:

H0: μ1=μ2=μ3=⋯=μkH_0:\ \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu_3 = \cdots = \mu_kH0​: μ1​=μ2​=μ3​=⋯=μk​

The alternate hypothesis is where people slip. It is simply:

H1: at least one of the sample means is not equalH_1:\ \text{at least one of the sample means is not equal}H1​: at least one of the sample means is not equal
Don't write H₁ as μ₁ ≠ μ₂ ≠ μ₃ ≠ ⋯

It's tempting to write the alternate as μ1≠μ2≠μ3≠⋯≠μk\mu_1 \neq \mu_2 \neq \mu_3 \neq \cdots \neq \mu_kμ1​=μ2​=μ3​=⋯=μk​ — but that's a stronger, wrong claim. That notation insists every mean differs from every other. ANOVA only needs at least one mean to stand apart from the rest. So we say it in words: at least one of the sample means is not equal.

The test statistic: the F-test​

The statistic ANOVA uses is the F-test, and its shape is beautifully simple:

F=variance between samplesvariance within samplesF = \frac{\text{variance between samples}}{\text{variance within samples}}F=variance within samplesvariance between samples​

The formula is short; the work behind it is not. Getting those two variances takes a lot of small steps — so we'll walk them carefully. First, what do "between" and "within" actually mean? Take three samples:

  • Variance within samples — inside a single group, how far its points scatter from their own group mean. In X1X_1X1​ the mean is 3, but 1 and 2 sit below it while 4 and 5 sit above — that spread around 3 is the within-group variance. This is the noise.

A worked one-way ANOVA, step by step​

This is the biggest test in the module — heavy on arithmetic, not on code. Follow the six steps once and it clicks.

A doctor wants to test a new medication that reduces headache. Participants are split into three conditions — 15 mg, 30 mg, 45 mg (the dosage is the factor's levels). Each patient then rates their headache from 1 to 10 (10 = headache completely gone). Are there differences between the three conditions, using α = 0.05?

Step 1 — Null and alternate hypotheses​

H0: μ15=μ30=μ45H1: not all means are equalH_0:\ \mu_{15} = \mu_{30} = \mu_{45} \qquad H_1:\ \text{not all means are equal}H0​: μ15​=μ30​=μ45​H1​: not all means are equal

Step 2 — Significance level​

α=0.05confidence interval=0.95\alpha = 0.05 \qquad \text{confidence interval} = 0.95α=0.05confidence interval=0.95

Step 3 — Degrees of freedom​

Three counts drive everything. The total sample size N=7×3=21N = 7 \times 3 = 21N=7×3=21; the per-group size n=7n = 7n=7; and a=a =a= the number of categories (groups) =3= 3=3.

dfbetween=a−1=3−1=2df_{\text{between}} = a - 1 = 3 - 1 = 2dfbetween​=a−1=3−1=2 dfwithin=N−a=21−3=18df_{\text{within}} = N - a = 21 - 3 = 18dfwithin​=N−a=21−3=18 dftotal=N−1=21−1=20df_{\text{total}} = N - 1 = 21 - 1 = 20dftotal​=N−1=21−1=20

Notice dfbetween+dfwithin=2+18=20=dftotaldf_{\text{between}} + df_{\text{within}} = 2 + 18 = 20 = df_{\text{total}}dfbetween​+dfwithin​=2+18=20=dftotal​ — the degrees of freedom partition just like the variance will. The pair we carry forward is (d1,d2)=(2,18)(d_1, d_2) = (2, 18)(d1​,d2​)=(2,18): the two numbers you look up in the F table.

Step 4 — Decision boundary​

The F-distribution is right-skewed (its tail is stretched to the right), so an ANOVA is one-tailed: the entire rejection region — area α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05 — is the right tail, leaving the 0.950.950.95 acceptance region to its left. Looking up the F table at d1=2d_1 = 2d1​=2, d2=18d_2 = 18d2​=18, α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05 gives the critical value:

CV=F2,18, 0.05=3.5546\text{CV} = F_{2,18,\,0.05} = 3.5546CV=F2,18,0.05​=3.5546

Decision rule: if the computed F is greater than 3.5546, reject the null hypothesis.

The F-distribution is right-skewed and non-negative, so an ANOVA is always one-tailed: the whole rejection region (area α) is the right tail. Read the critical value off the F table using two degrees of freedom — d₁ = a − 1 (between) and d₂ = N − a (within) — at your α, then reject H₀ if the computed F lands past it. For the worked example (d₁ = 2, d₂ = 18, α = 0.05 → 3.5546), F = 86.56 sails clear off the chart.

Step 5 — Compute the F-test statistic​

We build the ANOVA table one column at a time: SS (sum of squares) → df → MS (mean square) → F.

① Sum of squares between groups. With ∑ai\sum a_i∑ai​ = each group's total (57, 47, 21) and T=∑(∑ai)=125T = \sum(\sum a_i) = 125T=∑(∑ai​)=125 the grand total:

SSbetween=∑(∑ai)2n−T2N=572+472+2127−125221=842.71−744.05=98.67SS_{\text{between}} = \frac{\sum \left(\sum a_i\right)^2}{n} - \frac{T^2}{N} = \frac{57^2 + 47^2 + 21^2}{7} - \frac{125^2}{21} = 842.71 - 744.05 = 98.67SSbetween​=n∑(∑ai​)2​−NT2​=7572+472+212​−211252​=842.71−744.05=98.67

② Sum of squares within groups. Here ∑y2\sum y^2∑y2 squares every raw value and adds them (92+82+72+⋯=8539^2 + 8^2 + 7^2 + \cdots = 85392+82+72+⋯=853):

SSwithin=∑y2−∑(∑ai)2n=853−572+472+2127=853−842.71=10.29SS_{\text{within}} = \sum y^2 - \frac{\sum \left(\sum a_i\right)^2}{n} = 853 - \frac{57^2 + 47^2 + 21^2}{7} = 853 - 842.71 = 10.29SSwithin​=∑y2−n∑(∑ai​)2​=853−7572+472+212​=853−842.71=10.29

SStotal=SSbetween+SSwithin=98.67+10.29=108.96SS_{\text{total}} = SS_{\text{between}} + SS_{\text{within}} = 98.67 + 10.29 = 108.96SStotal​=SSbetween​+SSwithin​=98.67+10.29=108.96

Step 6 — Decision​

86.56>3.5546  ⇒  reject H086.56 > 3.5546 \;\Rightarrow\; \textbf{reject } H_086.56>3.5546⇒reject H0​

F lands far past the critical value — deep in the right tail (p-value ≈ 6×10−106\times10^{-10}6×10−10). Conclusion: there are differences between the three conditions. Not all the dosage means are equal — at least one differs from the others. Higher doses really do change the headache rating.

Why a quant cares​

Partitioning variance into between and within is not just an ANOVA ritual — it's the same decomposition that runs underneath most of quantitative finance:

  • Signal-to-noise is the whole game. F = between ÷ within is literally effect size ÷ dispersion — the same shape as a Sharpe ratio (mean edge ÷ volatility) or an information ratio. A strategy's mean return can look different across regimes, but if the within-regime scatter dwarfs the between-regime gap, the difference is noise. ANOVA formalizes exactly that judgement.
  • Variance decomposition is everywhere. The SStotal=SSbetween+SSwithinSS_{\text{total}} = SS_{\text{between}} + SS_{\text{within}}SStotal​=SSbetween​+SSwithin​ split is the ANOVA face of the regression identity explained + residual variance, and of risk attribution (systematic vs idiosyncratic). Once you see variance as something you partition rather than just measure, factor models and performance attribution read the same way.
  • F over a table of p-values. Testing three strategies (or venues, or signals) with one F-test controls the false-positive rate that many pairwise t-tests would inflate — the same discipline that keeps a research pipeline from data-mining spurious "winners."

The mechanics are heavy, but the idea is one line: real differences between groups are only convincing when they overwhelm the randomness inside them.
