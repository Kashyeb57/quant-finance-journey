## Geometric Brownian motion

GBM is a stochastic process which is widely used to model the behaviour of
assets (**stock price**).

GBM ensures that the asset price **remains positive** and incorporates
**randomness proportional to the current price level**, making it suitable for
quantitative modeling.

The stock-price margin contrasts **“−ve ?? ”** with **“0 or +ve”**. GBM with a
positive starting price stays strictly positive at finite times.

The notes say: **we use GBM to model stock price; we don't use it to model
interest rate**. The interest-rate branch instead points to **mean reversion
process → stochastic interest-rate model**. This describes the course's model
choices.

## The GBM equation

Start with the generalized Wiener equation:

$$
dX_t=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t.
$$

For GBM, the change is **proportional to the current value of Sₜ**:

$$
\frac{dS_t}{S_t}=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t.
$$

Therefore the **GBM equation**, an **SDE** to solve for modeling stock prices, is:

$$
\boxed{dS_t=\mu S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t}.
$$

- **μ:** the margin lists both **risk-free rate** and **stock's rate of return**.
  These correspond to risk-neutral pricing and a real-world return model,
  respectively; the spreadsheet below uses its specified drift.
- **σ:** volatility of the stock price.
- **Quant model → assumption**; **quant processes → assumption**.

### Randomness and ordinary differential equations

The reminder **dWₜ = Z × sqrt(t)** points to a **standard normal process**.
The margin also names **Poisson** and **Bernoulli** as other distributions;
they are not used for the standard-normal Z in this GBM construction.

$$
dX_t=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t.
$$

The comparison labels **DE / regular calculus** with the handwritten schematic
**dXₜ = μdt + σ**, and **SDE / random** with **dXₜ = μdt + σdWₜ**. The dWₜ
term is boxed as the **Wiener process**; an unfilled distribution reminder is
drawn as **[ ] N [ ]**.

## Solution of the stock-price SDE

$$
dS_t=\mu S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t.
$$

### Step 1: let's assume Yₜ = ln(Sₜ)

Instead of modeling Sₜ directly, let:

$$
Y_t=\ln(S_t).
$$

The **ln → +ve / −ve** margin is the reminder to take a real logarithm of a
**positive price**. The logarithm itself can be negative when the price is
between 0 and 1.

### Step 2: let's use Itô's lemma to solve

$$
dY_t=d\bigl(\ln(S_t)\bigr)
=\frac{1}{S_t}\,dS_t-\frac12\frac{1}{S_t^2}(dS_t)^2.
$$

The notes label the logarithm **stochastic in nature** and divide the right
side into **1st component** and **2nd component**.

### Step 3: substitute the SDE into dYₜ

$$
dS_t=\mu S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t.
$$

**1st component:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{S_t}\,dS_t
&=\frac{1}{S_t}\left(\mu S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t\right) \\
&=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t.
\end{aligned}
$$

**2nd component:**

$$
\frac12\frac{1}{S_t^2}(dS_t)^2
=\frac12\frac{1}{S_t^2}\left(\mu S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t\right)^2.
$$

The annotations say **ignore** the higher-order dt contributions: **dt → small
= 1/252**, **dt² → infinitely small = 1/(252)²**, with a zero-like oval beside
the latter. In Itô differential algebra, the rules being used are
**(dt)² = 0**, **dt dWₜ = 0**, and **(dWₜ)² = dt**; the finite number 1/(252)²
is small, not literally zero.

Thus:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac12\frac{1}{S_t^2}(dS_t)^2
&=\frac12\frac{1}{S_t^2}\left[\sigma S_t\,dW_t\right]^2 \\
&=\frac12\frac{1}{S_t^2}\left[\sigma^2 S_t^2(dW_t)^2\right] \\
&=\frac12\sigma^2(dW_t)^2 \\
&=\frac12\sigma^2\,dt.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Property of Wiener process:**

$$
\boxed{(dW_t)^2=dt}.
$$

**Combining the terms, we get:**

$$
dY_t=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t-\frac12\sigma^2\,dt.
$$

$$
\boxed{dY_t=\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)dt+\sigma\,dW_t}.
$$

### Step 4: integrate the above equation from 0 to t

$$
\int_0^t dY_s
=\int_0^t\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)ds
+\int_0^t\sigma\,dW_s.
$$

The two right-hand integrals are labelled **1st** and **2nd**. The first gives
the factor **t − 0**. For constant μ and σ, with W₀ = 0:

$$
Y_t-Y_0=\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)(t-0)+\sigma W_t.
$$

$$
\boxed{Y_t-Y_0=\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)t+\sigma W_t}.
$$

The margin explains **integrate = sum**, with small increments across **0 to t**:

$$
\int_0^t\sigma\,dW_s=\sigma W_t.
$$

It then returns to:

$$
Y_t=\ln(S_t),\qquad Y_0=\ln(S_0).
$$

:::note Integrated Wiener notation in the handwritten derivation

The source correctly evaluates the stochastic integral as **σWₜ**, but several
subsequent lines on pages 21–22 write **σdWₜ** instead. This includes the
displayed difference Yₜ − Y₀, both logarithm equations and the final exponential.
After integration over 0 to t, these terms are **σWₜ**, as used here. For one
simulation interval, the corresponding increment is **σ√Δt Z**.

:::

### Step 5: substitute Yₜ and Y₀ in the above equation

$$
\ln(S_t)-\ln(S_0)=\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)t+\sigma W_t.
$$

Use:

$$
\ln A-\ln B=\ln\left(\frac{A}{B}\right).
$$

$$
\ln\left(\frac{S_t}{S_0}\right)
=\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)t+\sigma W_t.
$$

### Step 6: taking exp on both sides

$$
\exp\left(\ln\left(\frac{S_t}{S_0}\right)\right)
=\exp\left[\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)t+\sigma W_t\right].
$$

$$
\frac{S_t}{S_0}=\exp\left[\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)t+\sigma W_t\right].
$$

$$
\boxed{S_t=S_0\exp\left[\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)t+\sigma W_t\right]}.
$$

The final annotation, continued by the single word on page 23, reads
**“equation to model stock price”**.

## Simulation of GBM

The instruction image in both workbooks says: **To simulate GBM in discrete
time, use:**

$$
S_{t+\Delta t}=S_t e^{(\mu-\sigma^2/2)\Delta t+\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}Z}.
$$

The increment instruction in the expanded workbook is:

$$
dW_t=\sqrt{dt}\,Z,
$$

where **Z ∼ N(0, 1)** is a standard normal random variable, and **dt** is the
time step. The accompanying bell curve illustrates that standard normal
distribution.

### Brownian Motion sheet

The headings are **Step**, **Random No**, **Delta T**, **Weiner Process**,
**Increment**; the expanded file also labels column F **dWt**.

**W0 = 0**. Under **Stand Norm Dist**, **Mean = 0**, **Var = 1**, and
**Sigma = SQRT(Var) = 1**.

| Cell | Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B2 | `=NORM.S.INV(RAND())` | Standard-normal random draw |
| C2 | `=SQRT(1/252)` | √Δt = 0.06299407883487121 |
| D2 | `=H1+C2*B2` | First process value, starting from zero |
| D3 | `=D2+C3*B3` | Previous value plus the next increment |
| E3 | `=D3-D2` | Increment recovered from consecutive values |
| F2, expanded file | `=B2*C2` | Increment directly from the random draw |

The formulas continue through **step 1000**. Excel stores `NORM.S.INV` with
the `_xlfn.` compatibility prefix. Despite the **Delta T** column label, C
contains **√Δt**; Δt itself is **1/252**.

The original notes beside the table read **Col B → Random Var**, **Wt+1 →
Wt + sigma * sqrt(t) * Z**, **sigma = 1**, **Z → Rand Var**, **T → Time**.
The expanded file writes **Wt+1 = Wt + sqrt(t) * Z**, **Wt+1 − Wt = sqrt(t)*Z**,
**dWt = Z * sqrt(t)**, and **Z → Standard Normal Rand Var**.

The first saved process value is **0.02728321027829932** in the first workbook
and **−0.05507000898776514** in the expanded one. Recalculating RAND produces
different simulations.
