## From Brownian motion to Wiener increments

The opening of the geometric Brownian motion notes repeats the
[Brownian motion lesson](./04-brownian-motion.mdx). Its variance derivation adds:

$$
\operatorname{Std}(X_t)=\sqrt{t}.
$$

The transition diagram is **Brownian motion → GBM / Black Scholes Modeling**.

**Wiener Process → Brownian Motion.** A Wiener process is a continuous
stochastic process that models random motion. **Stochastic → random**.

- Wiener process → **Wₜ**.
- Change in Wiener process → **dWₜ**.

The increment calculation is:

$$
W_{t+\Delta t}=W_t+\sigma\sqrt{dt}\,Z.
$$

$$
W_{t+\Delta t}-W_t=\sigma\sqrt{dt}\,Z.
$$

For the standard Wiener process, **σ = 1**:

$$
W_{t+\Delta t}-W_t=Z\sqrt{dt}.
$$

The notes write this as:

$$
\boxed{dW_t=Z\sqrt{dt}}.
$$

**Z → standard normal random variable**; **dt → change in time**. The wandering
path marks the change in the Wiener process as **Z√dt**. For a finite simulation
step, this notation means ΔW = Z√Δt.

## Generalized Wiener process

The generalized Wiener process is an extension of the standard Wiener process
that incorporates a **drift and volatility term**.

$$
\boxed{dW_t=Z\sqrt{dt}}\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{standard Wiener process}.
$$

### Mathematical formula of generalized Wiener process

The generalized Wiener process is defined by the **stochastic differential
equation (SDE)**:

$$
\boxed{dX_t=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t}.
$$

| Symbol | Meaning in the notes |
| --- | --- |
| Xₜ | Stochastic process that we are trying to model: stock price, interest rate |
| μ | Drift component; long-term average |
| σ | Diffusion component; represents volatility of the process |
| dWₜ | Standard Wiener-process increment or change |

The annotations label **μdt** as **drift**, **σ** as **vol**, and **dWₜ** as
the standard Wiener process, with the reminder **dWₜ = Z × sqrt(t)**. In this
increment formula, the time under the square root is the interval length.

The histogram sketch represents the normally distributed **dWₜ or increment**
and points to an **Excel spreadsheet**. The handwritten distribution is:

$$
dW_t\sim N(0,\sqrt{dt}).
$$

:::note Standard deviation versus variance

The expression above uses √dt as the spread parameter. In the earlier lesson,
the second argument of N is **variance**. Keeping that convention, the increment
is N(0, dt), with **standard deviation √dt**. The original notation is retained
above so these two conventions are not silently mixed.

:::

Under the annotation **“Differentiate”**, the notes repeat:

$$
dX_t=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t.
$$

For this generalized Wiener process, **μ = constant**, **σ = constant**, and
the simulation **dt = constant**. The example **μ = 5%** labels **μdt** as
**constant throughout**; dWₜ supplies the random part. A margin arrow says
**model stock price**.

## Itô's process

A further type of stochastic process is **“Itô's process”**. It generalizes the
Wiener process so the parameters **μ** and **σ** are functions of the underlying
variable **x** and time **t**:

$$
\boxed{dx=\mu(x,t)\,dt+\sigma(x,t)\,dW_t}.
$$

**x → underlying → stock**; **t → time**. Unlike the constant-parameter case,
μ and σ are **not necessarily constant**. The earlier constant form is recorded
in the margin as **μdt + σdWₜ**.

## Itô's lemma

Itô's lemma is a fundamental result in stochastic calculus which extends the
**classic chain rule of calculus to stochastic processes driven by Brownian
motion**.

Let Xₜ be a stochastic process which follows Itô's process:

$$
dX_t=\mu(X_t,t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t,t)\,dW_t.
$$

Let **f(Xₜ, t)** be a **twice differentiable function of Xₜ and t**. Then:

$$
df(X_t,t)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\,dt
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dX_t
+\frac12\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}\sigma^2(X_t,t)\,dt.
$$

**Substitute dXₜ**:

$$
df(X_t,t)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\,dt
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
\left(\mu(X_t,t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t,t)\,dW_t\right)
+\frac12\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}\sigma^2(X_t,t)\,dt.
$$

:::note Handwritten substitution correction

The intermediate substitution on page 14 writes **μdt + ½σ²dt** inside the
first-derivative term. The SDE above requires **μdt + σdWₜ** there. The final
boxed formula in the notes restores the stochastic term correctly; that final
formula is reproduced below.

:::

Collect terms to obtain:

$$
\boxed{
df(X_t,t)=
\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}
+\mu\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)dt
+\sigma\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dW_t
}.
$$
