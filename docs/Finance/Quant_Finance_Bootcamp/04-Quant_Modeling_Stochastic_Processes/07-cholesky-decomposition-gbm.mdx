## Correlated processes

**GBM → model a single stock.** Now consider **two stocks**:

- **S₁ → ABC**.
- **S₂ → XYZ**.

The example labels both as **financial companies** and asks whether S₁ and S₂
will have correlation or not: **yes**.

When we model our stocks using geometric Brownian motion, we need to make sure
that the **same correlation is also integrated within the GBM process**. The
diagram shows S₁ and S₂ linked by correlation before modeling, each passing
through a GBM arrow, with correlation retained between the two resulting
processes.

To integrate the correlation between the processes, we use **Cholesky
decomposition**.

## Cholesky decomposition

### Step 1: define the correlation matrix

The example's correlation between S₁ and S₂ is **ρ = 0.6 or 60%**.

$$
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{bmatrix}.
$$

**Note:**

$$
-1\leq\rho\leq1.
$$

### Step 2: define Cholesky decomposition

The goal is to find a **lower triangular matrix** such that:

$$
L=\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}&0\\L_{21}&L_{22}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
L^T=\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}&L_{21}\\0&L_{22}\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
\boxed{\Sigma=LL^T}.
$$

The superscript **T** marks the transpose; Σ is the **correlation matrix**.

### Matrix multiplication

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}&0\\L_{21}&L_{22}\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}&L_{21}\\0&L_{22}\end{bmatrix}.
$$

From the upper-left entry:

$$
L_{11}\cdot L_{11}=1,
\qquad L_{11}^2=1,
\qquad\boxed{L_{11}=1}.
$$

From the upper-right entry:

$$
L_{11}L_{21}+0\cdot L_{22}=\rho.
$$

$$
L_{11}L_{21}=\rho.
$$

Since **L₁₁ = 1**:

$$
\boxed{L_{21}=\rho}.
$$

From the lower-right entry:

$$
1=L_{21}^2+L_{22}^2.
$$

$$
1=\rho^2+L_{22}^2.
$$

$$
\boxed{L_{22}=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
$$

Therefore the **Cholesky matrix** is:

$$
L=\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}&0\\L_{21}&L_{22}\end{bmatrix}
=\boxed{\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\\rho&\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\end{bmatrix}}.
$$

## Use Cholesky decomposition in GBM

Let's assume we have **2 correlated stocks**.

### Step 1: GBM equation

For a single stock:

$$
dS_t=\mu S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t.
$$

- **Sₜ:** stock price at time t.
- **μ:** expected return.
- **σ:** volatility.
- **dWₜ:** Wiener process, the **random component**.

### Step 2: correlating our Wiener processes using Cholesky decomposition

The diagram connects **S₁, S₂ → ρ → dW₁, dW₂**, asks **“How?”**, and points
to **Cholesky decomposition**. It recalls **dW₁ = Z₁ sqrt(t)** for an increment
over a time interval.

**a) Generate standard normal random variables Z₁, Z₂.**

For each stock, the Excel draw is:

```excel
=NORM.S.INV(RAND())
```

**S₁ → Z₁**, **S₂ → Z₂**.

**b) Use Cholesky decomposition to transform these independent random variables
to correlated random variables.**

The two-column sketch labels the original Z₁/Z₂ columns **uncorrelated** and
the transformed Z₁′/Z₂′ columns **correlated random variables**, with **ρ**
beside the transformation arrow.

$$
L=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\\rho&\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad LL^T=\Sigma.
$$

### Calculate correlated Wiener processes

Using the source's primed dW notation:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}dW_1'\\dW_2'\end{bmatrix}
=L\begin{bmatrix}Z_1\\Z_2\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\\rho&\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}Z_1\\Z_2\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The uncorrelated dW₁/dW₂ are associated with Z₁/Z₂; multiplying by L gives the
correlated pair. The matrix multiplication gives:

$$
dW_1'=1\cdot Z_1+0\cdot Z_2,
\qquad\boxed{dW_1'=Z_1}.
$$

This is labelled **correlated Wiener process for S₁**.

$$
\boxed{dW_2'=\rho Z_1+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,Z_2}.
$$

This is labelled **correlated Wiener process for S₂**. The paired-column sketch
repeats **Z₁** in the first transformed column and **ρZ₁ + √(1−ρ²)Z₂** in the
second.

### Step 3: simulate GBM for correlated stocks

The handwritten equations are:

$$
S_{t+\Delta t}^{1}=S_t^{1}
\exp\left[(\mu_1-0.5\sigma_1^2)\Delta t+\sigma_1dW_1'\right],
$$

$$
S_{t+\Delta t}^{2}=S_t^{2}
\exp\left[(\mu_2-0.5\sigma_2^2)\Delta t+\sigma_2dW_2'\right].
$$

:::note Time-step scaling

The transformed variables **LZ** above are correlated standard-normal draws.
Actual Wiener increments over Δt are **√Δt LZ**. The source uses dW′ for both
the draws and the increments. Its Excel stock formulas supply the required
√Δt multiplier explicitly, as shown below.

:::

### Conclusion in the notes

The final diagram is **dW₁, dW₂ → dW₁′, dW₂′ → correlated Wiener process →
ρ → S₁, S₂**. The concluding equations repeat:

$$
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{bmatrix},
\qquad LL^T=\Sigma,
\qquad
\begin{bmatrix}dW_1'\\dW_2'\end{bmatrix}
=L\begin{bmatrix}Z_1\\Z_2\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
\boxed{dW_1'=Z_1,\qquad dW_2'=\rho Z_1+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,Z_2}.
$$

The copied M/N formulas retain **$B$8 / $B$9**, the initial stock prices, on

that the **GBM** sheet in the same workbook *does* compound correctly
(`L3 = L2*EXP(...)`), so the two sheets disagree.
