## Calculation for pricing European option using binomial tree

The handwritten calculation begins with a three-step tree. Each horizontal
interval is **Δt**; each branch multiplies the stock price by **u** or **d**.

| Time | Stock-price nodes, highest to lowest | Option-value labels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | S₀ | f |
| Δt | S₀u; S₀d | f_u; f_d |
| 2Δt | S₀uu; S₀ud; S₀dd | f_uu; f_ud; f_dd |
| 3Δt | S₀uuu; S₀uud; S₀udd; S₀ddd | Terminal payoffs |

Each node branches upward to its next **u** value and downward to its next
**d** value. Adjacent nodes share their middle successor. The later tree
highlights the final nodes and draws arrows **back toward f** for valuation;
at each local fork the successor option values are labelled **f_u** and **f_d**.

## Step 1: calculate the time steps

$$
\Delta t=\frac{T}{n}.
$$

This is the **length of each step**. The workbook instruction reads
“Calculate Time Step”: **Δt = T/n: Length of each time step**.

## Step 2: calculate up and down movement

The workbook calls this “Calculate Up and Down Factors”.

$$
u=e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}:
\quad\text{Upward movement factor}.
$$

$$
d=\frac{1}{u}
=\frac{1}{e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}}
=e^{-\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}:
\quad\text{Downward movement factor}.
$$

The one-step branch is **S₀ → S₀u** on the up move and **S₀ → S₀d** on the
down move.

## Step 3: calculate risk-neutral probabilities

**Probability of an upward move**:

$$
p=\frac{e^{r\Delta t}-d}{u-d}.
$$

**Probability of a downward move**: **1 − p**.

$$
p+(1-p)=1.
$$

## Step 4: construct the binomial tree

Create a binomial tree representing possible future prices of the underlying
asset at each node.

For each node **(i, j)** where **i** is the time step and **j** is the number
of upward moves:

$$
S_{i,j}=S_0\cdot u^j\cdot d^{(i-j)}.
$$

## Calculate the payoff

The handwritten PDF labels this **Step 4** again. In the workbook's seven-step
sequence, this is **Step 5: Calculate Option Payoff at Expiration**.

For each node at the final time step **(n, j)**, compute the payoff of the option.

For a European call option:

$$
\text{Call}=\max(S_T-K,0),
\qquad C_{n,j}=\max(S_{n,j}-K,0).
$$

For a European put option:

$$
\text{Put}=\max(K-S_T,0),
\qquad P_{n,j}=\max(K-S_{n,j},0).
$$

## Back propagation of option value

This is **Step 5** in the PDF and **Step 6: Back-Propagation of Option Values**
in the workbook.

Starting from the final time step and moving backward to the present, compute
the option value at each node as the discounted expected value of the option
in the next time step.

$$
f=e^{-r\Delta t}\left[p f_u+(1-p)f_d\right].
$$

For each node **(i, j)**:

**Call Option:**

$$
C_{i,j}=e^{-r\Delta t}\cdot
\left(p\cdot C_{i+1,j+1}+(1-p)\cdot C_{i+1,j}\right).
$$

**Put Option:**

$$
P_{i,j}=e^{-r\Delta t}\cdot
\left(p\cdot P_{i+1,j+1}+(1-p)\cdot P_{i+1,j}\right).
$$

## Step 7: result

The option price at the root of the tree **(0, 0)** gives the current price of
the European option:

$$
\text{Call Option Price}:\quad C_0=C_{0,0}.
$$

$$
\text{Put Option Price}:\quad P_0=P_{0,0}.
$$

### Results and the recorded market comparison

| Option | Model value | Screenshot quote | Sheet annotation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Call | 1.7130626117205423 | 0.4000 | Under Valued |
| Put | 6.986752561379506 | 9.95 | Over Valued |

“Under Valued” and “Over Valued” are the original sheet's own annotations,
comparing its model output against quotes recorded at the time on
**SPY240709C00555000** and the matching put. The call was quoted at **0.4000**,
**+0.2200 (+122.22%)** on the day, at close 4:14 PM EDT (**3:14 PM CT**); the
put at **9.95**, unchanged at **0.00 (0.00%)**, at close 3:08 PM EDT on July 1
(**2:08 PM CT**). Both were delayed OPR quotes in USD.

Treat the comparison as a lesson in model limits rather than a trade signal. A
five-step tree on one year of historical volatility says a call worth \$0.40 in
the market is worth \$1.71 — a gap of more than 4×. The tree is not finding
free money; it is telling you that five steps and backward-looking volatility
are a coarse instrument for a contract eight days from expiry, where the
market's own implied volatility is the better estimate.

:::note Two stock-node references in the source workbook

The put-tree stock cells **I30** (`=H29*B31`) and **J29** (`=I28*B31`) reference
the blank B31, producing saved values of **0**. The down-move factor they should
point at is **B15**. They are not used by the sheet's terminal payoff or backward
option-value formulas, so they do not change its reported put price — but they
are wrong, and worth knowing about if you rebuild the sheet and wonder why two
nodes read zero.

:::
