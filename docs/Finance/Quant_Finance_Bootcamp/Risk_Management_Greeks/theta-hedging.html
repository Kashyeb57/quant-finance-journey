Theta Hedging

Every hedge so far has defended against something that might or might not happen. Time decay is the one risk that is certain. The clock runs whether the market moves or not.

Definition​

Theta hedging is a risk-management strategy which is used to manage an option portfolio's exposure to time decay.

Recall what theta is: it measures the sensitivity of the option price with respect to time.

Theta decay​

As every day passes, the option price loses its value.

An option worth $2.50 today does not sit there:

DayOption price
D0D_0D0​$2.50
D1D_1D1​$2.40
D2D_2D2​$2.25
…falling

Nothing needs to happen in the market for this. The option is simply worth less because there is less time left for it to come good.

The goal​

The goal of theta hedging is to offset or reduce the impact of time decay on the portfolio value — to eliminate the impact of time decay on our option price.

Θp=0\Theta_p = 0Θp​=0

The sign of theta​

Theta's signs are the reverse of gamma's and vega's, and that reversal is the whole reason the hedge works:

ThetaPositions
PositiveShort call, short put
NegativeLong call, long put

The buyer of an option pays for time and watches it drain away; the seller collected that premium up front and keeps a little more of it each day. Decay is a cost to the long and income to the short.

Worked example​

You are long 100 call option contracts, with:

Θ=−50\Theta = -50Θ=−50

Theta is negative, which means every day the option price will lose its value — $50 a day, in this case, purely from the calendar.

Step 1 — find positive theta​

Theta hedging: select those option contracts whose theta is positive — a short call or a short put.

Suppose you enter a short position with:

Θ=+5\Theta = +5Θ=+5

Step 2 — size it​

Short 10 of them:

10×5=5010 \times 5 = \mathbf{50}10×5=50

Step 3 — total​

Θp=−50+50=0\Theta_p = -50 + 50 = \mathbf{0}Θp​=−50+50=0

Theta neutral. Every day that passes by, the option price will not change with time.

What the two legs are doing​

The structure is long call + short put, and the two legs are doing opposite things to the clock:

decline⏟long call  +  gain⏟short put  =  0\underbrace{\text{decline}}_{\text{long call}} \;+\; \underbrace{\text{gain}}_{\text{short put}} \;=\; \mathbf{0}long calldecline​​+short putgain​​=0

By adding a short position, your portfolio's net theta is zero. The decay you pay on the long leg is exactly the decay you collect on the short one.

The rule, in both directions​

Because the hedge is always the opposite side of the position you hold:

If the portfolio's theta is…Hedge with
Positivea long position (call or put) — long options carry negative theta
Negativea short position (call or put) — short options carry positive theta

Either way you end at Θp=0\Theta_p = 0Θp​=0.

When theta hedging is required​

It is needed at both ends of the maturity spectrum, for opposite reasons.

a) Long-term option contracts. If you plan to hold an option position for a longer period, theta hedging helps minimise the losses from time decay. Decay per day is small on a long-dated option, but you are exposed to it for a long time, and it accumulates.

b) Short-term option contracts. When you have a shorter term, theta decay accelerates quickly. A contract with one week left bleeds far faster per day than one with a month left — the decay curve steepens into expiry. Here theta hedging becomes very important, because the loss arrives fast.

The middle of the curve is where you can most afford to ignore it. The two ends are where it costs you.

Summary​

  • Theta hedging eliminates the effect of time on the portfolio value — Θp=0\Theta_p = 0Θp​=0 means the option price stops changing simply because a day passed.
  • Theta is positive for short positions (short call, short put) and negative for long positions (long call, long put) — decay is a cost to the buyer and income to the seller.
  • Worked: long 100 calls at Θ=−50\Theta = -50Θ=−50, neutralised by shorting 10 contracts at Θ=+5\Theta = +5Θ=+5 → −50+50=0-50 + 50 = 0−50+50=0.
  • The hedge is always the opposite side: positive portfolio theta is hedged long, negative portfolio theta is hedged short.
  • It matters for long-dated positions (decay accumulates) and for short-dated ones (decay accelerates into expiry).
