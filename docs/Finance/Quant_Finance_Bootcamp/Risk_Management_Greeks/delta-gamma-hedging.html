Delta-Gamma Hedging

Delta hedging kills direction but leaves gamma. Gamma hedging stabilises delta but leaves direction. Delta-gamma hedging does both.

Definition​

Delta-gamma neutral hedging is a risk-management strategy which aims to neutralise both delta and gamma.

Δp=0andΓp=0\Delta_p = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \Gamma_p = 0Δp​=0andΓp​=0

And each of the two conditions has a plain-English reading:

ConditionWhat it means
Delta neutral (Δp=0\Delta_p = 0Δp​=0)The portfolio's total delta is zero — it is not sensitive to small price changes in the underlying asset
Gamma neutral (Γp=0\Gamma_p = 0Γp​=0)The portfolio's total gamma is zero — delta does not change with a change in the underlying asset

Put together, the portfolio is protected against small and large moves. Delta neutrality handles the small ones; gamma neutrality is what keeps that protection standing when the move is big.

Worked example​

Long 200 call options on XYZ stock, with:

  • Delta = 0.4
  • Gamma = 0.05

Goal: make the portfolio delta-gamma neutral.

Step 1 — the portfolio's Greeks​

Δp=200×0.4=80Γp=200×0.05=10\Delta_p = 200 \times 0.4 = \mathbf{80} \qquad\qquad \Gamma_p = 200 \times 0.05 = \mathbf{10}Δp​=200×0.4=80Γp​=200×0.05=10

Step 2 — make the portfolio delta neutral​

Delta is +80, so sell 80 shares (delta −80):

Portfolio=long 200 calls  +  short 80 stock\text{Portfolio} = \text{long 200 calls} \;+\; \text{short 80 stock}Portfolio=long 200 calls+short 80 stock Δp=80−80=0⇒ delta neutral\Delta_p = 80 - 80 = \mathbf{0} \quad \Rightarrow\ \textbf{delta neutral}Δp​=80−80=0⇒ delta neutral

Step 3 — make the portfolio gamma neutral​

The portfolio gamma is still +10, and stock contributes nothing to it. To cancel +10 we need −10, and negative gamma comes only from the short side:

GammaPosition
PositiveLong call, long put
NegativeShort call, short put

From the market we find a call option contract which, taken short, carries:

Δ=−0.3Γ=−0.05\Delta = -0.3 \qquad\qquad \Gamma = -0.05Δ=−0.3Γ=−0.05

Short 200 of them:

200×(−0.05)=−10200 \times (-0.05) = \mathbf{-10}200×(−0.05)=−10

The portfolio's gamma, leg by leg:

Γp=+10⏟long 200 calls  +  0⏟short 80 stock  +  −10⏟short 200 calls  =  0\Gamma_p = \underbrace{+10}_{\text{long 200 calls}} \;+\; \underbrace{0}_{\text{short 80 stock}} \;+\; \underbrace{-10}_{\text{short 200 calls}} \;=\; \mathbf{0}Γp​=long 200 calls+10​​+short 80 stock0​​+short 200 calls−10​​=0

Gamma neutrality achieved.

Step 4 — the option leg broke the delta, so fix it again​

This is the step people forget. Entering that call option contract changed the portfolio's delta, because the option we just shorted carries delta as well as gamma:

Δp=200×(−0.3)⏟the new short calls  +  0⏟already-neutral portfolio=−60\Delta_p = \underbrace{200 \times (-0.3)}_{\text{the new short calls}} \;+\; \underbrace{0}_{\text{already-neutral portfolio}} = \mathbf{-60}Δp​=the new short calls200×(−0.3)​​+already-neutral portfolio0​​=−60

We were at zero; we are now at −60. So buy 60 shares (+60 delta):

Δp=−60+60=0\Delta_p = -60 + 60 = \mathbf{0}Δp​=−60+60=0

And buying stock does not disturb gamma, because stock has none. Final state:

Δp=0andΓp=0\Delta_p = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \Gamma_p = 0Δp​=0andΓp​=0

That is delta-gamma hedging.

The ordering rule​

The example teaches a sequence, and the sequence is not arbitrary:

  1. Hedge gamma first, with options. Only an option can change gamma.
  2. Hedge delta second, with stock. Stock changes delta and nothing else.

The reason is that the two instruments are not symmetric:

InstrumentAffects deltaAffects gamma
Stock✅ (±1 per share)❌ (always 0)
Option✅✅

Because the option leg moves both Greeks, it must be sized first — from the gamma requirement, which stock cannot help with. The stock leg is then a pure correction: it mops up whatever delta the option leg left behind, without touching the gamma you just fixed.

Do it the other way around — delta first, then gamma — and the gamma trade knocks your delta back out of neutral, which is exactly what happened between Step 2 and Step 4 above. Step 2 was not wasted (it is how the bookkeeping is kept legible), but the final delta number can only be computed once the option leg is set.

Summary​

  • Delta-gamma hedging neutralises both Greeks at once: Δp=0\Delta_p = 0Δp​=0 and Γp=0\Gamma_p = 0Γp​=0, protecting against small and large moves in the underlying.
  • Delta neutral = insensitive to small price changes. Gamma neutral = delta itself stops changing.
  • Worked: long 200 calls at Δ\DeltaΔ 0.4 / Γ\GammaΓ 0.05 → Δp=80\Delta_p = 80Δp​=80, Γp=10\Gamma_p = 10Γp​=10; short 80 stock; short 200 calls at Δ\DeltaΔ −0.3 / Γ\GammaΓ −0.05 to zero the gamma; then buy 60 stock to re-zero the delta the option leg introduced.
  • Gamma is hedged with options, delta with stock — and because options carry delta too, the option leg is sized first and the stock leg last.
