The [CIR theory note](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Stochastic_Interest_Rate_Modeling/cir-theory)
adds one term to Vasicek. This note builds it on the same data, and the
interesting part is how little of the workbook changes — and where that turns
out to be a problem.

## Calibration

The `CIR Parameter Est (MLE)` sheet is laid out identically to the
[Vasicek one](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Stochastic_Interest_Rate_Modeling/vasicek-excel-calibration):
252 observations of the 3-month Treasury rate, with the same seven-column
pipeline.

| Col | Label | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D | Actual | `=C2/100` |
| F | r(t)−r(t−1) | `=D2-D3` |
| G | **dr (CIR)** | `=$O$2*($O$3-D3)*$O$7` |
| H | residual | `=F2-G2` |
| I | pdf | `=NORMDIST(H2,0,$O$4,FALSE)` |
| J | ln(pdf) | `=LN(I2)` |
| L2 | Sum | `=SUM(J2:J252)` |

Column G is $a(b - r_{t-1})\,dt$ — **the same as Vasicek**, which is right,
because CIR and Vasicek share a drift. The square root lives only in the
diffusion.

### The calibrated parameters

| Parameter | Vasicek | **CIR** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $a$ — mean reversion speed | 0.10232 | **11.07860** |
| $b$ — long-term mean | 0.05741 | **0.05490** |
| $\sigma$ — volatility | 0.00018280 | **0.00018093** |
| Log-likelihood | 1804.10 | **1806.81** |

Two things stand out.

**The two $\sigma$ values are almost identical** — 0.00018093 against
0.00018280, a 1% difference. For models whose diffusion terms differ by a factor
of $\sqrt{r_t} \approx 0.235$, that is suspicious. It is the first sign of the
problem below.

**The two $a$ values are not remotely similar.** Vasicek fits 0.102, CIR fits
11.08 — a factor of over 100, on the same data. In half-life terms:

| | Half-life $\ln 2 / a$ |
| --- | --- |
| Vasicek | **6.8 years** |
| CIR | **16 trading days** |

One model says the rate reverts over years, the other over three weeks. Both
"fit" the data about equally well (log-likelihoods 1804 and 1807). That is
**weak identification** — as noted in the Vasicek note, one year of data
contains very little information about mean reversion, so the optimiser is free
to land almost anywhere without the likelihood objecting.

### The Feller check

The theory note flagged that CIR's non-negativity guarantee requires the
**Feller condition**:

$$
2ab \ge \sigma^2
$$

With the fitted parameters:

$$
2ab = 2 \times 11.0786 \times 0.054897 = 1.2164
\qquad\qquad
\sigma^2 = 3.27 \times 10^{-8}
$$

Comfortably satisfied — by seven orders of magnitude. **The simulated rate
cannot reach zero**, so CIR's headline property holds here.

## The likelihood forgot the square root

Look again at column I, in both workbooks:

```excel
Vasicek:  =NORMDIST(H2, 0, $O$4, FALSE)
CIR:      =NORMDIST(H2, 0, $O$4, FALSE)
```

They are **the same formula.** But under CIR the residual is not
$\sigma\,dW_t$ — it is $\sigma\sqrt{r_t}\,dW_t$, so its standard deviation
**varies with the rate level** and the density should be

$$
\text{NORMDIST}\left(H,\; 0,\; \sigma\sqrt{r_{t-1}}\sqrt{\Delta t},\; \text{FALSE}\right)
$$

Because the $\sqrt{r_t}$ is missing, the sheet is fitting a **Vasicek
likelihood** and labelling the answer $\sigma_{CIR}$. That is why the two
volatilities came out nearly equal — they are estimates of the same thing.

The consequence appears when the simulation *does* apply the square root:

| | Value |
| --- | --- |
| $\sigma$ consistent with the data, given $\sqrt{r_0}$ | **0.00077863** |
| $\sigma$ actually fitted | **0.00018093** |
| Understated by | **4.3×** |

## Simulation

The `CIR Simulation (MLE)` sheet is the Vasicek simulation with one insertion —
**10 paths across E:N, 252 days down the rows**, all starting at
$r_0 = 0.0552$:

```excel
E3 = $B$1*($B$2-E2)*$B$6 + $B$3*SQRT(E2)*SQRT($B$6)*NORM.S.INV(RAND()) + E2
```

Set against the Vasicek version, the difference is exactly `SQRT(E2)`:

$$
r_{t+\Delta t} = a(b - r_t)\Delta t + \sigma\underbrace{\sqrt{r_t}}_{\text{CIR only}}\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z + r_t
$$

`E2` is the previous row's rate, so `SQRT(E2)` is $\sqrt{r_t}$ — the theory
implemented literally, and correctly.

:::danger The two defects compound — CIR paths come out 68× too smooth

This sheet inherits the **units mismatch** documented in the Vasicek note (a
per-day $\sigma$ multiplied by $\sqrt{\Delta t}$ a second time), **and** adds
the missing-$\sqrt{r}$ calibration error above. Together:

| | Daily shock std |
| --- | --- |
| Actual 3-month rate | **0.00018294** |
| Vasicek simulation | 0.00001152 — **15.9×** too smooth |
| **CIR simulation** | **0.00000268** — **68×** too smooth |

The 68 is the 15.9 from the units mismatch multiplied by the 4.3 from the
missing $\sqrt{r_t}$ in the likelihood.

**To fix it properly**, both ends have to agree: put $\sqrt{r_{t-1}}\sqrt{\Delta t}$
into the calibration's `NORMDIST` so the fitted $\sigma$ is a true annualised
CIR volatility, and leave the simulation formula as it stands.

The *structure* of both sheets is right — the columns, the mean reversion, the
square root in the simulation, the Feller-safe parameters. It is the scaling
that does not survive the trip from one sheet to the other.

:::

## Module 5, end to end

| | Vasicek | CIR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Equation** | $dr_t = a(b-r_t)dt + \sigma\,dW_t$ | $dr_t = a(b-r_t)dt + \sigma\sqrt{r_t}\,dW_t$ |
| **Rates produced** | Positive **and negative** | **Positive only** |
| **Fits which economy** | Germany, Japan | US, India, China |
| **Calibrated $a$** | 0.1023 (6.8-year half-life) | 11.08 (16-day half-life) |
| **Calibrated $b$** | 0.0574 | 0.0549 |
| **Log-likelihood** | 1804.10 | 1806.81 |

The module's real lesson is in that last row. **Two models with materially
different dynamics fit the same year of Treasury data almost equally well**, and
their mean-reversion speeds differ by a factor of 100. Choosing between them
cannot be done on goodness-of-fit alone — it has to be done on the **structural
question the theory note posed**: can the rate you are modelling go negative or
not?

That is the honest state of short-rate modelling. The maths is exact; the
calibration is not.

## Summary

- The CIR calibration sheet is **structurally identical** to the Vasicek one —
  same seven columns, same drift in column G, since **CIR and Vasicek share a
  drift.**
- Calibrated: **$a$ = 11.0786, $b$ = 0.054897, $\sigma$ = 0.00018093**,
  log-likelihood **1806.81**.
- **The Feller condition holds easily** ($2ab = 1.22$ vs $\sigma^2 = 3.3\times10^{-8}$),
  so the simulated rate stays positive.
- **The likelihood omits $\sqrt{r_t}$** — it is a Vasicek likelihood, which is
  why the two fitted volatilities are nearly equal, and it understates
  $\sigma_{CIR}$ by **4.3×**.
- Combined with the inherited **units mismatch**, CIR's simulated paths are
  **68× less volatile** than the data.
- The simulation's `SQRT(E2)` is the theory implemented correctly — the
  **scaling**, not the structure, is what breaks.
- **$a$ differs by 100× between the two models on identical data** while the
  log-likelihoods barely move: mean reversion is **weakly identified** in one
  year of observations.
