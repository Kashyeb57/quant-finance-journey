Monte Carlo VaR in Excel

The most elaborate sheet in the workbook, and the one where the theory actually needs all its machinery: 1,000 simulations of three correlated stocks.

Inputs​

Estimated from the same 251 daily returns:

Volatility σ\sigmaσ =STDEV.S(...)0.016255970.017164900.01821889

The volatilities match the variance-covariance sheet exactly, as they should — same data, same estimator. Weights are again 0.3 / 0.4 / 0.3 on $1M.

The correlation matrix and its Cholesky factor​

Σ=[10.664611170.819878380.6646111710.752924000.819878380.752924001]\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0.66461117 & 0.81987838 \\ 0.66461117 & 1 & 0.75292400 \\ 0.81987838 & 0.75292400 & 1 \end{bmatrix}Σ=​10.664611170.81987838​0.6646111710.75292400​0.819878380.752924001​​

The sheet builds LLL with the standard recursion, one cell per entry:

AS15 = SQRT(AS10)                          ' L11
AS16 = AS11/AS15                           ' L21
AT16 = SQRT(AT11 - AS16^2)                 ' L22
AS17 = AS12/AS15                           ' L31
AT17 = (AT12 - AS17*AS16)/AT16             ' L32
AU17 = SQRT(AU12 - AS17^2 - AT17^2)        ' L33
L=[1000.664611170.7471894000.819878380.278408220.50028822]L = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0.66461117 & 0.74718940 & 0 \\ 0.81987838 & 0.27840822 & 0.50028822 \end{bmatrix}L=​10.664611170.81987838​00.747189400.27840822​000.50028822​​

Verified independently: LLTLL^TLLT reproduces Σ\SigmaΣ to eight decimal places. This is the 2×2 case from the theory note generalised to three assets, and it is built correctly.

The simulation chain​

The single array formula in AC6 is elegant: MMULT multiplies the whole 1000×3 block of independent draws by LLL in one operation, turning the entire simulation correlated at a stroke.

Why the two methods nearly agree here, but not in the historical sheet

In the historical sheet the same two approaches differed by $527. Here they differ by $5.58 — nearly a hundred times less.

The reason is sample size. PERCENTILE.EXC interpolates at rank p(n+1)p(n+1)p(n+1):

nnnp(n+1)p(n+1)p(n+1) at 99%Interpolates
Historical2512.5252% of the way between order stats 2 and 3
Monte Carlo1,00010.011% of the way between order stats 10 and 11

With 1,000 observations the tail is densely packed and the interpolation barely moves. More simulations make the choice of estimator stop mattering — which is one more argument for running a lot of them.

The live formulas no longer reproduce the reported VaR

The sorted column BN — the one both VaR blocks read from — contains hardcoded numbers, not formulas. The live calculation chain (Q → AC → AX → BH → BK) still runs, and it produces something very different:

Standard deviation of portfolio returns
Frozen values in BN (used for VaR)0.016325
Live chain in BK (recalculates)0.001003
Historical portfolio σ\sigmaσ (cov matrix)0.015624

The frozen values are right — 0.0163 against a historical 0.0156 is ordinary sampling variation on 1,000 draws, and their mean (0.000723) matches the portfolio mean (0.000737).

The live chain is wrong by a factor of about 252\sqrt{252}252​. Sorting what it currently produces would give:

CIReported (frozen)Live chain would give
99%−$35,447.27−$2,430.01
95%−$27,201.83−$1,720.68
90%−$20,114.71−$1,280.47

The cause. μ\muμ and σ\sigmaσ come from AVERAGE and STDEV.S of daily returns — they are already per-day quantities. But the GBM formula then applies a time step of 1/2521/2521/252:

S0exp⁡[(μdaily−σdaily22)×1252+σdaily Z1252]S_0 \exp\left[\left(\mu_{\text{daily}} - \frac{\sigma_{\text{daily}}^2}{2}\right)\times\frac{1}{252} + \sigma_{\text{daily}}\, Z \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{252}}\right]S0​exp[(μdaily​−2σdaily2​​)×2521​+σdaily​Z2521​​]

In daily units, one day is dt=1dt = 1dt=1, not 1/2521/2521/252. Either drop the 1/2521/2521/252 and the 1/252\sqrt{1/252}1/252​, or annualise first (μann=252μd\mu_{ann} = 252\mu_dμann​=252μd​, σann=252 σd\sigma_{ann} = \sqrt{252}\,\sigma_dσann​=252​σd​) and keep them. Doing neither shrinks the shock by 252≈15.9\sqrt{252} \approx 15.9252​≈15.9.

This is the same units error found in the Module 4 Monte Carlo sheets and the Module 5 simulation sheets — a per-period parameter re-scaled as though it were annual. It is the single most common defect in this course's workbooks.

What to take from the sheet: the structure — the Cholesky construction, the MMULT correlation step, the sort-and-rank blocks — is correct and worth copying. The frozen results are correct. The live GBM line is not.

The three methods, finally compared​

1-day VaR @99% CI95% CI90% CI
Variance-Covariance−$35,609.28−$24,961.76−$19,285.60
Historical Simulation−$36,253.51−$24,160.85−$19,101.28
Monte Carlo Simulation−$35,447.27−$27,201.83−$20,114.71
Spread$806 (2.2%)$3,041 (12.6%)$1,013 (5.3%)

At 99% the three agree within about 2% — three quite different assumptions converging on the same answer, which is the reassuring case.

At 95% they do not: Monte Carlo says $27,202 where historical says $24,161. The reason is instructive. Monte Carlo draws from a normal distribution, so its 95% quantile sits where normality says it should. The historical method uses the actual returns, whose shape near the 5% mark apparently differs from normal. Variance-covariance, which also assumes normality, lands close to Monte Carlo — as you would expect from two methods sharing an assumption.

So the disagreement is not noise: it is the normality assumption showing up, and it shows up more at 95% than at 99% on this data.

Summary​

  • 1,000 simulations of three correlated stocks, driven by GBM, on the same JPM/MS/BAC portfolio.
  • The 3×3 Cholesky factor is built correctly and verified — LLTLL^TLLT reproduces the correlation matrix exactly.
  • One array formula, TRANSPOSE(MMULT(L, TRANSPOSE(draws))), correlates all 3,000 draws at once.
  • Rank and percentile methods differ by only $5.58 here versus $527 in the historical sheet — because 1,000 observations make the interpolation irrelevant.
  • The sorted returns are frozen values; the live formula chain is 252\sqrt{252}252​ too small because daily μ\muμ and σ\sigmaσ are scaled by 1/2521/2521/252. Re-sorting the live output would report $2,430 instead of $35,447.
  • Across all three methods the 99% figures agree within 2.2%, but the 95% figures differ by 12.6% — the normality assumption, made visible.
