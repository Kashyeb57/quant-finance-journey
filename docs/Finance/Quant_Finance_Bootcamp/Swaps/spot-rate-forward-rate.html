Spot Rates and Forward Rates

Valuing a swap means discounting future cash flows, and the floating leg's future cash flows are not yet known. Both problems need the term structure of interest rates — which is what this note builds.

Spot rate​

The spot rate is the current interest rate at which a financial instrument can be bought or sold for immediate delivery.

Usage: it is used for immediate transactions.

The parallel the notes draw: stock ABC trading at $100 right now is the spot value of the stock. An interest rate of 4% available right now — a SOFR quote, say — is the spot rate. Both are prices for doing something today.

Forward rate​

The forward rate is an agreed-upon rate between 2 parties for a transaction that will occur at a specified future date.

0⟶3⏟spot rate3⟶6⏟forward rate\underbrace{0 \longrightarrow 3}_{\text{spot rate}} \qquad \underbrace{3 \longrightarrow 6}_{\text{forward rate}}spot rate0⟶3​​forward rate3⟶6​​

Note: the forward rate is not a market rate today, but rather a rate expected in the future.

⤷ Derived from the spot rate.

That last line is the important one, and the rest of this note is why it is true.

Used for
Spot ratesimmediate transactions
Forward ratesfuture transactions

Deriving the forward rate from spot rates​

The tool is continuous compounding:

Compounding=ertDiscounting=e−rt\text{Compounding} = e^{rt} \qquad\qquad \text{Discounting} = e^{-rt}Compounding=ertDiscounting=e−rt

where rrr is the rate and ttt the time.

The setup​

Two ways to invest an amount PPP from time 0 to time T2T_2T2​:

Case 1 — invest straight through. Put PPP in at the T2T_2T2​ spot rate R2R_2R2​ and hold to T2T_2T2​:

FVT2=P⋅eR2T2— (1)FV_{T_2} = P \cdot e^{R_2 T_2} \qquad\qquad \text{— (1)}FVT2​​=P⋅eR2​T2​— (1)

Case 2 — invest in two steps. Put PPP in at the T1T_1T1​ spot rate R1R_1R1​ until T1T_1T1​:

FVT1=P⋅eR1T1FV_{T_1} = P \cdot e^{R_1 T_1}FVT1​​=P⋅eR1​T1​

then roll that amount forward from T1T_1T1​ to T2T_2T2​ at the forward rate FFF, for a duration of (T2−T1)(T_2 - T_1)(T2​−T1​):

FVT2=FVT1×eF(T2−T1)=P⋅eR1T1⋅eF(T2−T1)— (2)FV_{T_2} = FV_{T_1} \times e^{F(T_2 - T_1)} = P \cdot e^{R_1 T_1} \cdot e^{F(T_2-T_1)} \qquad \text{— (2)}FVT2​​=FVT1​​×eF(T2​−T1​)=P⋅eR1​T1​⋅eF(T2​−T1​)— (2)

Why they must be equal​

If the two routes gave different amounts, you could borrow via the cheaper one and lend via the richer one and pocket the difference with no capital and no risk. The notes name it directly: arbitrage opportunities.

$100→    1yr, then 1yr    X$100→        2yr straight        X\$100 \xrightarrow{\;\;1\text{yr, then 1yr}\;\;} X \qquad \$100 \xrightarrow{\;\;\;\;2\text{yr straight}\;\;\;\;} X$1001yr, then 1yr​X$1002yr straight​X

Both must land on the same XXX. So set (1) = (2):

P eR2T2=P eR1T1⋅eF(T2−T1)\cancel{P}\,e^{R_2 T_2} = \cancel{P}\,e^{R_1 T_1} \cdot e^{F(T_2-T_1)}P​eR2​T2​=P​eR1​T1​⋅eF(T2​−T1​) eR2T2=eR1T1⋅eF(T2−T1)e^{R_2 T_2} = e^{R_1 T_1} \cdot e^{F(T_2-T_1)}eR2​T2​=eR1​T1​⋅eF(T2​−T1​)

Take logs of both sides, using log⁡(A×B)=log⁡(A)+log⁡(B)\log(A \times B) = \log(A) + \log(B)log(A×B)=log(A)+log(B), and since log and exp are inverses:

R2T2=R1T1+F(T2−T1)R_2 T_2 = R_1 T_1 + F(T_2 - T_1)R2​T2​=R1​T1​+F(T2​−T1​) F(T2−T1)=R2T2−R1T1F(T_2 - T_1) = R_2 T_2 - R_1 T_1F(T2​−T1​)=R2​T2​−R1​T1​   F=R2T2−R1T1T2−T1  \boxed{\;F = \frac{R_2 T_2 - R_1 T_1}{T_2 - T_1}\;}F=T2​−T1​R2​T2​−R1​T1​​​

the forward rate between T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​, where R1R_1R1​ and R2R_2R2​ are the zero rates / spot rates for those maturities.

A slip in the middle of the derivation

Immediately after stating the log rule correctly, the source writes

log⁡[eR2T2]=log⁡[eR1T1+eF(T2−T1)]\log\left[e^{R_2T_2}\right] = \log\left[e^{R_1T_1} + e^{F(T_2-T_1)}\right]log[eR2​T2​]=log[eR1​T1​+eF(T2​−T1​)]

with a plus inside the bracket where the previous line correctly had a product. The very next line then gives the right answer, R2T2=R1T1+F(T2−T1)R_2T_2 = R_1T_1 + F(T_2-T_1)R2​T2​=R1​T1​+F(T2​−T1​) — which only follows from the product form.

It is a transcription slip mid-derivation; the result is correct. But the distinction matters: the sum appears after taking the log, not before.

Reading the formula​

Rearranged, the formula says something intuitive:

R2T2=R1T1+F(T2−T1)R_2 T_2 = R_1 T_1 + F(T_2 - T_1)R2​T2​=R1​T1​+F(T2​−T1​)

Total growth over the long period = growth over the short period + growth over the stub. The forward rate is whatever rate makes the second leg fill exactly the gap the two spot rates leave.

Which also explains why forward rates can look extreme. If R2R_2R2​ is barely above R1R_1R1​ but T2T_2T2​ is much larger than T1T_1T1​, the implied FFF has to be low — and if the curve is steep, FFF can exceed both spot rates.

Summary​

  • Spot rate: the rate available now, for immediate transactions.
  • Forward rate: an agreed rate for a transaction at a specified future date — not a market rate today, but one derived from spot rates.
  • Continuous compounding is erte^{rt}ert; discounting is e−rte^{-rt}e−rt.
  • The derivation equates investing straight through to T2T_2T2​ against investing to T1T_1T1​ then rolling forward — they must agree or there is an arbitrage.
  • F=R2T2−R1T1T2−T1\boxed{F = \dfrac{R_2T_2 - R_1T_1}{T_2 - T_1}}F=T2​−T1​R2​T2​−R1​T1​​​
  • Equivalently R2T2=R1T1+F(T2−T1)R_2T_2 = R_1T_1 + F(T_2-T_1)R2​T2​=R1​T1​+F(T2​−T1​): the long period's growth is the short period's growth plus the forward stub.
