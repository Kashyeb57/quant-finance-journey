Monte Carlo — Asian Option

This is the contract Monte Carlo exists for. An Asian option has no closed-form solution, so the European note's check against Black-Scholes was the last time an independent answer was available. From here, simulation is the answer.

Where it sits​

TypeContracts
Standard contractEuropean, American
Exotic contractAsian, Barrier, Digital

And Monte Carlo handles all of them — European, Asian, Barrier.

Definition​

Asian options are exotic options where the payoff depends on the average price of the underlying asset over a specified period.

The contrast is the whole idea:

Unlike a European option, which considers the price of the stock at expiry / maturity, an Asian option reduces the risk and uncertainty by taking the average price of the stock.

The payoffs​

European — the terminal price only:

C=max⁡(ST−K, 0)P=max⁡(K−ST, 0)C = \max(S_T - K,\, 0) \qquad\qquad P = \max(K - S_T,\, 0)C=max(ST​−K,0)P=max(K−ST​,0)

where STS_TST​ is the stock price at maturity.

Asian — the average instead:

  C=max⁡(Savg−K, 0)    P=max⁡(K−Savg, 0)  \boxed{\;C = \max(S_{avg} - K,\, 0)\;} \qquad\qquad \boxed{\;P = \max(K - S_{avg},\, 0)\;}C=max(Savg​−K,0)​P=max(K−Savg​,0)​

where SavgS_{avg}Savg​ is the average of the stock price over all the time steps:

Savg=a+b+c+d+e+f6S_{avg} = \frac{a + b + c + d + e + f}{6}Savg​=6a+b+c+d+e+f​

A worked path​

A path generated by GBM visits these prices over six steps to maturity:

ttt123456
Price100101102103104105
Savg=100+101+102+103+104+1056=102.5S_{avg} = \frac{100 + 101 + 102 + 103 + 104 + 105}{6} = \mathbf{102.5}Savg​=6100+101+102+103+104+105​=102.5

A European option on this path would settle on 105. The Asian settles on 102.5. Same path, different number — and the difference is the point of the contract.

Each column steps forward recursively under GBM. Then each column collapses to a single number:

Path 1→SavgPath 2→Savg⋯Path 10,000→Savg\text{Path 1} \rightarrow S_{avg} \qquad \text{Path 2} \rightarrow S_{avg} \qquad \cdots \qquad \text{Path 10,000} \rightarrow S_{avg}Path 1→Savg​Path 2→Savg​⋯Path 10,000→Savg​

Each SavgS_{avg}Savg​ becomes a payoff:

C=max⁡(Savg−K, 0)C = \max(S_{avg} - K,\, 0)C=max(Savg​−K,0)

and from there it is the standard Monte Carlo ending — average, then discount:

Avg payoff=Payoff1+Payoff2+⋯+Payoff10,00010,000\text{Avg payoff} = \frac{\text{Payoff}_1 + \text{Payoff}_2 + \cdots + \text{Payoff}_{10{,}000}}{10{,}000}Avg payoff=10,000Payoff1​+Payoff2​+⋯+Payoff10,000​​ Discounted payoff=e−rT[Avg payoff]\text{Discounted payoff} = e^{-rT}\left[\text{Avg payoff}\right]Discounted payoff=e−rT[Avg payoff]

which is the price of the Asian call option.

Note where the only change lives: one extra averaging step per path, inserted between the simulation and the payoff. Everything else — GBM, average across paths, discount — is identical to the European case. That is how cheaply Monte Carlo absorbs path dependence.

Arithmetic versus geometric averaging​

The notes are careful that "average" is ambiguous, and both forms are used.

Arithmetic average:

Savg=S1+S2+S3+⋯+S66S_{avg} = \frac{S_1 + S_2 + S_3 + \cdots + S_6}{6}Savg​=6S1​+S2​+S3​+⋯+S6​​

Geometric average:

Savg=(S1×S2×S3×S4×S5×S6)1/6S_{avg} = \left(S_1 \times S_2 \times S_3 \times S_4 \times S_5 \times S_6\right)^{1/6}Savg​=(S1​×S2​×S3​×S4​×S5​×S6​)1/6

which generalises to the geometric mean:

Geometric mean=(∏i=1nxi)1/n\text{Geometric mean} = \left(\prod_{i=1}^{n} x_i\right)^{1/n}Geometric mean=(i=1∏n​xi​)1/n

where ∏\prod∏ means product — the multiplicative counterpart of ∑\sum∑.

And for a one-year Asian option, n=252n = 252n=252 trading days:

(S1×S2×S3×⋯×S252)1/252\left(S_1 \times S_2 \times S_3 \times \cdots \times S_{252}\right)^{1/252}(S1​×S2​×S3​×⋯×S252​)1/252

The Excel sheet​

Same contract, same layout as the European sheet — 100 paths across columns O:DJ, 6 daily steps down rows 3-8 — with one row inserted:

CellLabelFormula
K5Number of steps (days)6
K6No. of sims100
O3:O8The GBM pathsteps forward from O2
O9Average stock price=AVERAGE(O3:O8)
O10Option payoff (call)=MAX(O9-$B$6,0)
O11Option payoff (put)=MAX($B$6-O9,0)
O12Average payoff (call)=AVERAGE(O10:DJ10)
O13Discounted price (call)=O12*EXP(-B8*B7)
O14 / O15The same two steps for the put

Row 9 is the entire Asian modification. The European sheet reads O8, the terminal price; the Asian sheet reads AVERAGE(O3:O8). One cell.

Note also that the average runs O3:O8 and excludes O2 — the starting price S0S_0S0​ is not part of the average, only the six simulated steps are. That is correct: S0S_0S0​ is known, not sampled.

This sheet inherits the European sheet's drift error

The per-step formula is the same one flagged in the European note: it multiplies the drift by SQRT(N3) instead of N3, and uses a daily mean return where the risk-free rate belongs. The averaging logic above is sound — the path generator underneath it is not.

Does averaging really reduce risk?​

The notes claim it does. It is checkable, and it is true — with the risk-neutral generator, 400,000 paths on the SPY contract:

Std dev
Terminal price STS_TST​9.41
Path average SavgS_{avg}Savg​6.10

The average is 35% less variable than the terminal price. Averaging six draws cancels some of the noise in each — the same reason a sample mean is steadier than a single observation.

And because an option is priced on uncertainty, less uncertainty means a cheaper option:

CallPut
European1.70636.9642
Asian0.60606.1748

The Asian call costs about a third of the European. That is not a mispricing — it is the contract being genuinely less exposed. An Asian option cannot be made valuable by one lucky day at expiry, and it cannot be ruined by one unlucky one.

Which is also why they exist commercially: a corporate hedging a year of fuel purchases cares about the average price it paid, not the price on one arbitrary December afternoon. The contract matches the exposure.

Summary​

  • Asian options are exotic: the payoff depends on the average price of the underlying over a period, not the terminal price.
  • C=max⁡(Savg−K, 0)C = \max(S_{avg} - K,\, 0)C=max(Savg​−K,0) and P=max⁡(K−Savg, 0)P = \max(K - S_{avg},\, 0)P=max(K−Savg​,0).
  • Worked: a path visiting 100…105 averages to 102.5, where a European would settle on 105.
  • The average can be arithmetic or geometric, the latter being (∏xi)1/n\left(\prod x_i\right)^{1/n}(∏xi​)1/n — 252 factors for a one-year contract.
  • In the simulation, the only change is one averaging step per path, before the payoff. Average across paths and discount exactly as before.
  • In Excel the whole modification is =AVERAGE(O3:O8) in place of reading the terminal cell — and S0S_0S0​ is correctly excluded.
  • Averaging cuts variability by ~35%, which makes the Asian call worth about a third of the equivalent European. Less uncertainty, cheaper option.
