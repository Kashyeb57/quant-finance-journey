Generalized Wiener Process & Itô's Lemma

From Brownian motion to Wiener increments​

The opening of the geometric Brownian motion notes repeats the Brownian motion lesson. Its variance derivation adds:

Std⁡(Xt)=t.\operatorname{Std}(X_t)=\sqrt{t}.Std(Xt​)=t​.

The transition diagram is Brownian motion → GBM / Black Scholes Modeling.

Wiener Process → Brownian Motion. A Wiener process is a continuous stochastic process that models random motion. Stochastic → random.

  • Wiener process → Wₜ.
  • Change in Wiener process → dWₜ.

The increment calculation is:

Wt+Δt=Wt+σdt Z.W_{t+\Delta t}=W_t+\sigma\sqrt{dt}\,Z.Wt+Δt​=Wt​+σdt​Z. Wt+Δt−Wt=σdt Z.W_{t+\Delta t}-W_t=\sigma\sqrt{dt}\,Z.Wt+Δt​−Wt​=σdt​Z.

For the standard Wiener process, σ = 1:

Wt+Δt−Wt=Zdt.W_{t+\Delta t}-W_t=Z\sqrt{dt}.Wt+Δt​−Wt​=Zdt​.

The notes write this as:

dWt=Zdt.\boxed{dW_t=Z\sqrt{dt}}.dWt​=Zdt​​.

Z → standard normal random variable; dt → change in time. The wandering path marks the change in the Wiener process as Z√dt. For a finite simulation step, this notation means ΔW = Z√Δt.

Generalized Wiener process​

The generalized Wiener process is an extension of the standard Wiener process that incorporates a drift and volatility term.

dWt=Zdt⟶standard Wiener process.\boxed{dW_t=Z\sqrt{dt}}\quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{standard Wiener process}.dWt​=Zdt​​⟶standard Wiener process.

Mathematical formula of generalized Wiener process​

The generalized Wiener process is defined by the stochastic differential equation (SDE):

dXt=μ dt+σ dWt.\boxed{dX_t=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t}.dXt​=μdt+σdWt​​.
SymbolMeaning in the notes
XₜStochastic process that we are trying to model: stock price, interest rate
μDrift component; long-term average
σDiffusion component; represents volatility of the process
dWₜStandard Wiener-process increment or change

The annotations label μdt as drift, σ as vol, and dWₜ as the standard Wiener process, with the reminder dWₜ = Z × sqrt(t). In this increment formula, the time under the square root is the interval length.

The histogram sketch represents the normally distributed dWₜ or increment and points to an Excel spreadsheet. The handwritten distribution is:

dWt∼N(0,dt).dW_t\sim N(0,\sqrt{dt}).dWt​∼N(0,dt​).
Standard deviation versus variance

The expression above uses √dt as the spread parameter. In the earlier lesson, the second argument of N is variance. Keeping that convention, the increment is N(0, dt), with standard deviation √dt. The original notation is retained above so these two conventions are not silently mixed.

Under the annotation “Differentiate”, the notes repeat:

dXt=μ dt+σ dWt.dX_t=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t.dXt​=μdt+σdWt​.

For this generalized Wiener process, μ = constant, σ = constant, and the simulation dt = constant. The example μ = 5% labels μdt as constant throughout; dWₜ supplies the random part. A margin arrow says model stock price.

Itô's process​

A further type of stochastic process is “Itô's process”. It generalizes the Wiener process so the parameters μ and σ are functions of the underlying variable x and time t:

dx=μ(x,t) dt+σ(x,t) dWt.\boxed{dx=\mu(x,t)\,dt+\sigma(x,t)\,dW_t}.dx=μ(x,t)dt+σ(x,t)dWt​​.

x → underlying → stock; t → time. Unlike the constant-parameter case, μ and σ are not necessarily constant. The earlier constant form is recorded in the margin as μdt + σdWₜ.

Itô's lemma​

Itô's lemma is a fundamental result in stochastic calculus which extends the classic chain rule of calculus to stochastic processes driven by Brownian motion.

Let Xₜ be a stochastic process which follows Itô's process:

dXt=μ(Xt,t) dt+σ(Xt,t) dWt.dX_t=\mu(X_t,t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t,t)\,dW_t.dXt​=μ(Xt​,t)dt+σ(Xt​,t)dWt​.

Let f(Xₜ, t) be a twice differentiable function of Xₜ and t. Then:

df(Xt,t)=∂f∂t dt+∂f∂x dXt+12∂2f∂x2σ2(Xt,t) dt.df(X_t,t)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\,dt +\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dX_t +\frac12\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}\sigma^2(X_t,t)\,dt.df(Xt​,t)=∂t∂f​dt+∂x∂f​dXt​+21​∂x2∂2f​σ2(Xt​,t)dt.

Substitute dXₜ:

df(Xt,t)=∂f∂t dt+∂f∂x(μ(Xt,t) dt+σ(Xt,t) dWt)+12∂2f∂x2σ2(Xt,t) dt.df(X_t,t)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\,dt +\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \left(\mu(X_t,t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t,t)\,dW_t\right) +\frac12\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}\sigma^2(X_t,t)\,dt.df(Xt​,t)=∂t∂f​dt+∂x∂f​(μ(Xt​,t)dt+σ(Xt​,t)dWt​)+21​∂x2∂2f​σ2(Xt​,t)dt.
Handwritten substitution correction

The intermediate substitution on page 14 writes μdt + ½σ²dt inside the first-derivative term. The SDE above requires μdt + σdWₜ there. The final boxed formula in the notes restores the stochastic term correctly; that final formula is reproduced below.

Collect terms to obtain:

df(Xt,t)=(∂f∂t+μ∂f∂x+σ22∂2f∂x2)dt+σ∂f∂x dWt.\boxed{ df(X_t,t)= \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} +\mu\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} +\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)dt +\sigma\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dW_t }.df(Xt​,t)=(∂t∂f​+μ∂x∂f​+2σ2​∂x2∂2f​)dt+σ∂x∂f​dWt​​.

The final stochastic term continues at the top of the next page. The notes label this “Mathematical Derivation — Itô's Lemma”, with “1 hr” beside it and a reminder “Upload 1 video — Itô lemma derivation”. No such video is included among the supplied Module 4 files.

Itô's lemma → to calculate how we can model stock price.
