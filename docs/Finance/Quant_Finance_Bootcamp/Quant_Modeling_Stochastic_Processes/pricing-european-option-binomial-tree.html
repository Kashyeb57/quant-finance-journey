Quant Modeling: Pricing European Option Using Binomial Tree

Calculation for pricing European option using binomial tree​

The handwritten calculation begins with a three-step tree. Each horizontal interval is Δt; each branch multiplies the stock price by u or d.

TimeStock-price nodes, highest to lowestOption-value labels
0S₀f
ΔtS₀u; S₀df_u; f_d
2ΔtS₀uu; S₀ud; S₀ddf_uu; f_ud; f_dd
3ΔtS₀uuu; S₀uud; S₀udd; S₀dddTerminal payoffs

Each node branches upward to its next u value and downward to its next d value. Adjacent nodes share their middle successor. The later tree highlights the final nodes and draws arrows back toward f for valuation; at each local fork the successor option values are labelled f_u and f_d.

Step 1: calculate the time steps​

Δt=Tn.\Delta t=\frac{T}{n}.Δt=nT​.

This is the length of each step. The workbook instruction reads “Calculate Time Step”: Δt = T/n: Length of each time step.

Step 2: calculate up and down movement​

The workbook calls this “Calculate Up and Down Factors”.

u=eσΔt:Upward movement factor.u=e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}: \quad\text{Upward movement factor}.u=eσΔt​:Upward movement factor. d=1u=1eσΔt=e−σΔt:Downward movement factor.d=\frac{1}{u} =\frac{1}{e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}} =e^{-\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}: \quad\text{Downward movement factor}.d=u1​=eσΔt​1​=e−σΔt​:Downward movement factor.

The one-step branch is S₀ → S₀u on the up move and S₀ → S₀d on the down move.

Step 3: calculate risk-neutral probabilities​

Probability of an upward move:

p=erΔt−du−d.p=\frac{e^{r\Delta t}-d}{u-d}.p=u−derΔt−d​.

Probability of a downward move: 1 − p.

p+(1−p)=1.p+(1-p)=1.p+(1−p)=1.

Step 4: construct the binomial tree​

Create a binomial tree representing possible future prices of the underlying asset at each node.

For each node (i, j) where i is the time step and j is the number of upward moves:

Si,j=S0⋅uj⋅d(i−j).S_{i,j}=S_0\cdot u^j\cdot d^{(i-j)}.Si,j​=S0​⋅uj⋅d(i−j).

Calculate the payoff​

The handwritten PDF labels this Step 4 again. In the workbook's seven-step sequence, this is Step 5: Calculate Option Payoff at Expiration.

For each node at the final time step (n, j), compute the payoff of the option.

For a European call option:

Call=max⁡(ST−K,0),Cn,j=max⁡(Sn,j−K,0).\text{Call}=\max(S_T-K,0), \qquad C_{n,j}=\max(S_{n,j}-K,0).Call=max(ST​−K,0),Cn,j​=max(Sn,j​−K,0).

For a European put option:

Put=max⁡(K−ST,0),Pn,j=max⁡(K−Sn,j,0).\text{Put}=\max(K-S_T,0), \qquad P_{n,j}=\max(K-S_{n,j},0).Put=max(K−ST​,0),Pn,j​=max(K−Sn,j​,0).

Back propagation of option value​

This is Step 5 in the PDF and Step 6: Back-Propagation of Option Values in the workbook.

Starting from the final time step and moving backward to the present, compute the option value at each node as the discounted expected value of the option in the next time step.

f=e−rΔt[pfu+(1−p)fd].f=e^{-r\Delta t}\left[p f_u+(1-p)f_d\right].f=e−rΔt[pfu​+(1−p)fd​].

For each node (i, j):

Call Option:

Ci,j=e−rΔt⋅(p⋅Ci+1,j+1+(1−p)⋅Ci+1,j).C_{i,j}=e^{-r\Delta t}\cdot \left(p\cdot C_{i+1,j+1}+(1-p)\cdot C_{i+1,j}\right).Ci,j​=e−rΔt⋅(p⋅Ci+1,j+1​+(1−p)⋅Ci+1,j​).

Put Option:

Pi,j=e−rΔt⋅(p⋅Pi+1,j+1+(1−p)⋅Pi+1,j).P_{i,j}=e^{-r\Delta t}\cdot \left(p\cdot P_{i+1,j+1}+(1-p)\cdot P_{i+1,j}\right).Pi,j​=e−rΔt⋅(p⋅Pi+1,j+1​+(1−p)⋅Pi+1,j​).

Step 7: result​

The option price at the root of the tree (0, 0) gives the current price of the European option:

Call Option Price:C0=C0,0.\text{Call Option Price}:\quad C_0=C_{0,0}.Call Option Price:C0​=C0,0​. Put Option Price:P0=P0,0.\text{Put Option Price}:\quad P_0=P_{0,0}.Put Option Price:P0​=P0,0​.

Historical returns and volatility​

The SPY history has columns Date, Adj Close, and Returns, covering 252 prices from 2023-06-30 through 2024-07-01. The first price is 437.165192 and the last is 545.340027.

The sheet records both return definitions:

  • Return = Price Today/Price Yes -1
  • Return = ln(Price Today/Price Yes)

The actual Returns column uses the first definition. AC3 is =AB3/AB2-1, copied down through AC253. Its first return is 0.0011505033090557504.

Results and the recorded market comparison​

OptionModel valueScreenshot quoteSheet annotation
Call1.71306261172054230.4000Under Valued
Put6.9867525613795069.95Over Valued

“Under Valued” and “Over Valued” are the original sheet's own annotations, comparing its model output against quotes recorded at the time on SPY240709C00555000 and the matching put. The call was quoted at 0.4000, +0.2200 (+122.22%) on the day, at close 4:14 PM EDT (3:14 PM CT); the put at 9.95, unchanged at 0.00 (0.00%), at close 3:08 PM EDT on July 1 (2:08 PM CT). Both were delayed OPR quotes in USD.

Treat the comparison as a lesson in model limits rather than a trade signal. A five-step tree on one year of historical volatility says a call worth $0.40 in the market is worth $1.71 — a gap of more than 4×. The tree is not finding free money; it is telling you that five steps and backward-looking volatility are a coarse instrument for a contract eight days from expiry, where the market's own implied volatility is the better estimate.

Two stock-node references in the source workbook

The put-tree stock cells I30 (=H29*B31) and J29 (=I28*B31) reference the blank B31, producing saved values of 0. The down-move factor they should point at is B15. They are not used by the sheet's terminal payoff or backward option-value formulas, so they do not change its reported put price — but they are wrong, and worth knowing about if you rebuild the sheet and wonder why two nodes read zero.
