Three derivative types have appeared so far in this course — **forwards**,
**futures** and **options**. This module covers the fourth: **swaps.**

## Definition

> Swaps are **financial derivatives that allow two parties to exchange a
> sequence of cash flows over a specified period based on predetermined terms.**

**Cash flow** here means simply **the flowing of cash between two parties.**

Party A and Party B agree that, on a schedule, A pays B something and B pays A
something else. What makes it a *derivative* is that the amounts are not fixed
sums — they are **derived** from something that moves.

> Typically, a swap involves swapping **cash flows which are based on variables
> such as interest rates, currencies, equities**, etc.

## Types of swaps

| | Type |
| --- | --- |
| a) | **Interest Rate Swap** |
| b) | **Currency Swap** |
| c) | **Equity Swap** |

This module covers the first two — the
[interest rate swap](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Swaps/interest-rate-swap-part-2)
in depth, and the
[currency swap](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Swaps/currency-swap)
at the end.

## How a swap is structured

> A swap involves **2 counterparties that agree to exchange cash flow over a
> certain period of time.**

> The exact terms of the swap agreement are **negotiated by the counterparties**
> and then **formalised in a legal contract.**

That word *negotiated* is doing real work. A swap is not a standardised product
you buy off a screen — it is a bilateral agreement, written to fit two specific
parties.

### What the terms must specify

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What is to be swapped?** | which cash flows, on which variable |
| **Between whom is the swap?** | the two counterparties |
| **Notional amount** | e.g. **\$100M** |
| **Maturity of the swap** | how long it runs |
| **Any contingencies** | including the **day count convention** |

:::note The notional is notional

The **notional amount** is the number the interest payments are *calculated on*
— it is generally never exchanged in an interest rate swap. A \$100M notional
swap does not involve \$100M changing hands; it involves the *difference between
two interest calculations* on \$100M changing hands.

The [currency swap](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Swaps/currency-swap)
is the exception — there the principal genuinely is exchanged, because the two
sides are in different currencies.

:::

## Who uses swaps

> Swaps are mainly used by **institutional investors** such as **banks** and
> other **financial institutions**, **governments**, **corporations** and
> **investment banks.**

And the purpose is consistent:

$$
\textbf{Manage risk} \longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{Interest rate risk} \
\text{Currency risk} \
\text{Equity risk}
\end{cases}
$$

A corporation with floating-rate debt and a fixed-rate budget has a mismatch. A
swap converts one into the other without retiring the debt.

## Why you have never seen one quoted

Two facts that follow directly from the "negotiated bilaterally" structure:

> **Swaps do not trade on exchanges.**

> **Retail investors do not engage in swaps.**

**Swaps are always traded OTC** — over the counter. Because each contract is
negotiated to fit its two counterparties, there is nothing standardised to list.
That is the trade-off: complete flexibility in the terms, at the cost of no
central market, no exchange price, and — as
[Module 1 covered](/docs/Forwards-and-Futures)
— **counterparty risk** that an exchange-cleared product would not carry.

## Summary

- A **swap** lets **two parties exchange a sequence of cash flows** over a set
  period on **predetermined terms**, based on variables like **interest rates,
  currencies or equities.**
- Three types: **interest rate, currency, equity.**
- Terms are **negotiated bilaterally and formalised in a legal contract** —
  what is swapped, between whom, the **notional amount**, the **maturity**, and
  contingencies such as the **day count convention.**
- The **notional is a calculation base**, not an amount that changes hands (the
  currency swap excepted).
- Used by **institutions to manage interest rate, currency and equity risk.**
- **Always OTC** — never exchange-traded, and not used by retail investors.
