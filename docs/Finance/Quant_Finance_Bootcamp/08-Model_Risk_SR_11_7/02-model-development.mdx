> **Model risk management begins with robust model development, implementation
> and use.** Another essential element is **sound model validation.**

Note the order. Validation comes *second*. You cannot validate your way out of a
badly built model — which is why this section comes before
[Section 5](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Model_Risk_SR_11_7/model-validation).

The third leg is **model governance**, which

> sets an **effective framework with defined roles and responsibilities** for
> **clear communication of model assumptions and limitations.**

## What an effective development process starts with

**a) A clear objective — a statement of purpose.**

**b) Design, theory and logic underlying the model**, which should be

> **well documented and generally supported by published research and sound
> industry practice.**

That documentation has to cover four things:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Mathematical specification** | the equations themselves |
| **Numerical technique** | how they are solved |
| **Approximation** | what shortcuts were taken |
| **Strengths / weaknesses** | → the model's **limitations** |

> The **developer should ensure that the components of the model are appropriate
> for the intended business use case.**

## Consider the alternatives

> **Comparison with alternate theories and approaches is a fundamental component
> of a sound modelling process.**

This is a requirement people routinely skip. It is not enough to build $M_1$ and
show it works — you have to be able to say why $M_1$ and not $M_2$, $M_3$ or
$M_4$:

$$
M_1 \;\checkmark \longrightarrow \text{implemented}
\qquad
\underbrace{M_2,\; M_3,\; M_4}_{\text{considered and rejected}} \longrightarrow \textbf{justify}
$$

This module has already done exactly that several times — the
[three VaR methods](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Value_at_Risk/var-monte-carlo-excel)
disagreeing by 12.6% at 95%, or
[Vasicek versus CIR](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Stochastic_Interest_Rate_Modeling/cir-excel)
fitting the same data with mean-reversion speeds 100× apart. In both cases the
choice between models mattered and could not be settled by fit alone. **Writing
down why you chose one is the documentation SR 11-7 is asking for.**

## Data

> **Rigorous assessment of data quality and relevance** is required.

> The **developer should be able to demonstrate that the data and information
> are suitable for the model** and that **they are consistent with the use
> case.**

The lecturer's shorthand is the old one, and it is exactly right:

$$
\textbf{“Garbage In, Garbage Out”}
$$

### When the data does not exist

> If data **isn't available, data proxies are used** — something with the **same
> behaviour.**

But a proxy is a substitution, and substitutions are where risk hides:

> Proxies must be **carefully identified, justified and documented.**

Otherwise the proxy itself becomes a source of **model risk** — the model looks
fine, and the thing it is actually measuring is not the thing you think.

## Testing

> **Testing various components of the model, and its overall functioning, is
> evaluated to determine whether the model is performing as intended or not.**

And which tests apply depends entirely on what the model assumes:

> **Statistical tests depend on specific distribution assumptions.**

**For a linear regression model:**

| # | Test |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Linearity** — QQ plot |
| 2 | **Homoskedasticity** |
| 3 | **Autocorrelation between residuals** |
| 4 | **Multicollinearity** — VIF |
| 5 | **Normality of residuals** |

**For a time series model:**

| # | Test |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test** |
| 2 | **KPSS** |

**For a Value at Risk model:**

| # | Test |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | [**Kupiec test**](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Value_at_Risk/backtest-kupiec) |
| 2 | [**Traffic Light Approach**](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Value_at_Risk/backtest-traffic-light) |
| 3 | **Christoffersen test** |
| 4 | **Binomial test** |

### Never rely on one test

> **Different tests have different strengths and weaknesses under different
> conditions.**
>
> **Do not rely on a single test.**
>
> Use a **variety of tests to develop a sound model.**

:::note This is the exact gap Module 6 identified

The [Kupiec note](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Value_at_Risk/backtest-kupiec)
ended on a limitation: Kupiec counts breaches but **cannot see whether they
clustered.** Three breaches spread across a year and three on consecutive days
score identically — and the second is far more alarming.

**Christoffersen's test is the one that closes that gap**, by testing
*independence* of breaches rather than just their number. Which is precisely why
SR 11-7 lists all four together and says not to rely on any single one. The
guidance is not being vague; it is describing a real blind spot that a second
test covers.

:::

## Model use

> **Models should be used only for the purpose for which they were designed and
> validated.**

$$
\text{Black-Scholes} \longrightarrow \text{European option} \;\checkmark
$$

> **Misusing a model beyond its intended scope leads to incorrect decisions and
> heightened risk** → **model risk.**

### What is required of users

> **Users must understand the assumptions, limitations and weaknesses of the
> model.**

> **Users must interpret the model output correctly and critically.**

> This includes **understanding the sensitivity of the result to input changes
> and assumptions.**

:::note Why the guidance cares about the reader, not just the model

The deck makes this concrete with a small scenario. A model reports
**VaR = \$5M.** Two people read that number:

| | Reads it as |
| --- | --- |
| **User A** (technical background) | a **potential loss** at a stated confidence level |
| **User B** | a **profit** figure |

Same output, opposite conclusions. **The model was correct in both cases.**

This is why "model risk" is defined as arising from *incorrect **or misused***
outputs. A validated model handed to someone who misreads it produces exactly
the same adverse decision as a broken one — and no amount of validation
prevents it. Only documentation, training and clear reporting do.

:::

## Summary

- **MRM begins with development, implementation and use** — validation is the
  *second* line of defence, not the first.
- A sound build starts with a **clear objective** and **documented design,
  theory and logic**, supported by **published research and industry practice**,
  covering **specification, numerical technique, approximations and
  limitations.**
- **Comparison with alternative approaches is required** — justify why this
  model and not another.
- **Data must be assessed for quality and relevance** ("garbage in, garbage
  out"), and **proxies must be identified, justified and documented.**
- **Test against the model's own assumptions** — regression diagnostics, ADF and
  KPSS for time series, four different backtests for VaR.
- **Never rely on a single test**; different tests fail in different ways.
  Kupiec counts breaches, **Christoffersen catches clustering.**
- **Use the model only as designed**, and **users must understand its
  assumptions and interpret output critically** — a correct model misread
  produces the same bad decision as a wrong one.
