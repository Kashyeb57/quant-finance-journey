Good models and rigorous validation are not enough on their own. Someone has to
own the framework, staff it, and be accountable when it fails. That is
governance.

> **Model risk governance is provided at the highest level by the Board of
> Directors (BOD) and Senior Management (SM)**, when they establish a
> **bank-wide approach to model risk management.**

> **It fits into the broader risk management of the organisation.**

## The seven components

| # | Component |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Board of Directors and Senior Management** |
| 2 | **Policies and Procedures** |
| 3 | **Roles and Responsibilities** |

| 7 | **Documentation** |

## 1 — Board of Directors and Senior Management

The **BOD delegates to Senior Management**, whose duties are:

- **Establishing policies and procedures**
- **Establishing compliance**
- **Assigning competent staff**
- **Overseeing model development**
- **Overseeing model implementation**
- **Evaluating results**
- **Overseeing model validation**

And the board's own responsibility is stated in risk terms:

> **Board members must ensure that model risk is within the tolerance limit.**

That phrasing matters — it treats model risk the same way a board treats credit
or market risk: something with a stated appetite that must be kept inside it,
not a technical matter delegated away entirely.

## 2 — Policies and Procedures

> **All aspects of model risk management should be covered by suitable
> policies.**

Those policies must cover:

- **Model and model risk definitions**
- **Acceptable practices for model development**
- **Assessment of model risk**
- **Appropriate model validation**
- **Model governance**

> **Policies should identify the roles and responsibilities within the MRM
> framework**, with clear detail on:
>
> - **staff expertise**
> - **authority**
> - **reporting lines**

Note the recurrence of *authority* and *reporting lines* — the same requirement
that made
[validation independence](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Model_Risk_SR_11_7/model-validation)
meaningful. A policy that names a validator without granting them standing
achieves nothing.

## 3 — Roles and Responsibilities

> The roles in MRM can be divided into **ownership, control and compliance.**

> **Business units are responsible for the model risk associated with their
> business strategies.**

That is the important allocation: model risk belongs to **the business that uses
the model**, not to the quantitative team that built it.

### The model owner

> The model owner holds **accountability for the model** — its

- **performance**
- **development**
- **implementation**
- **use**
- **validation**

One named person is answerable for the whole lifecycle. Without that, a model
with problems has no natural home and drifts.

## 4 — Internal Audit

> A bank's **internal audit should assess the overall effectiveness of the MRM
> framework.**

:::note Audit checks the framework, not the models

The guidance is careful here, and the distinction is easy to miss:

> **Internal audit should not replicate MRM activities**, but instead **evaluate
> whether the MRM is comprehensive, rigorous and effective.**

Validation asks *is this model right?* Internal audit asks *is the validation
function itself working?* Duplicating the validators' work would waste the
audit's independence on a question already being answered.

> **Staff that look at the effectiveness of MRM should be expert in their field
> of work.**

:::

What internal audit checks:

- **Procedures for updating models are clearly documented**
- **Check model owners are meeting standards or not**
- **Data is reliable or not**
- **Review validation activities**

## 5 — External Resources

> **Although MRM is an internal process, a bank can also decide to engage
> external resources for the MRM framework** — for **developing, implementing,
> validating**, or **model governance**.

But engaging outside help does not transfer responsibility:

> Whenever external resources are used:
>
> - the **scope of work should be clearly written and agreed**
> - there must be a **designated internal team which should look upon the work
>   conducted by the external resources**

## 6 — Model Inventory

> **Banks should maintain a comprehensive set of information for:**
>
> - **models implemented**
> - **models under development**
> - **models retired** — the ones **not being used now**

> A **team maintains a firm-wide inventory of all the models**, in order to
> **evaluate the model risk in aggregate.**

That last phrase is the reason the inventory exists. The
[overview note](/docs/Finance/Quant_Finance_Bootcamp/Model_Risk_SR_11_7/overview)
established that **aggregate model risk is affected by interaction and
dependencies among models** — one model's output feeding another's input. You
cannot see that exposure model-by-model. **Only a firm-wide inventory makes the
dependency graph visible.**

### What the inventory records

- **Purpose of the model**
- **Product for which the model is designed and used**
- **Any limitations**
- **List of input variables and output variables**
- **Function of the model**
- **When the model was last updated**

## 7 — Documentation

Documentation runs through everything above, and the guidance's position is
consistent: **if it is not written down, it did not happen.** Model choice,
assumptions, data, limitations, validation findings, deficiencies, and the
reasons validation could not be performed all have to be recorded.

## Vendor and third-party models

A financial institution buying in **vendor data** or a **vendor model /
software** takes on model risk with it.

> **Banks should ensure that there are appropriate processes for selecting
> vendor models.**

> **Banks require the vendor to provide development evidence** — the model's
> **design** (input, processing, output) and **intended use** — so the bank can
> judge whether **the model is appropriate for the bank or not.**

> **Limitations and assumptions must be documented clearly by the vendor.**

**When the vendor will not share** the mathematical formula, implementation
code, back end, or adjustments — which is common, since these are trade secrets
— the bank must compensate with its own testing:

$$
\text{Sensitivity analysis} \qquad \text{Benchmarking} \qquad \text{Stress testing}
$$

The notes name real systems in this category: **QRM**, **Polypath** and
**Calypso** in banking, **Aladdin** (BlackRock), and **ADCO** for mortgage.

> The bank must still **conduct ongoing monitoring and outcome analysis** on a
> vendor model.

**A black box does not exempt you from validation — it changes the technique.**
If you cannot inspect the internals, you test the behaviour.

## Section 7 — Conclusion

> **Banks should comply with a strong MRM framework**, covering:

| |
| --- |
| **Model development** |
| **Model implementation** |
| **Validation** |
| **Model governance** |

Which is the whole document in four lines, and the four notes of this module.

## Summary

- **Model risk governance sits with the Board and Senior Management**, who set a
  **bank-wide approach** that fits into the organisation's broader risk
  management. The **board must keep model risk within its tolerance limit.**
- **Seven components**: board/senior management, policies and procedures, roles
  and responsibilities, internal audit, external resources, model inventory,
  documentation.
- **Policies must cover** definitions, development practices, risk assessment,
  validation and governance — with explicit **staff expertise, authority and
  reporting lines.**
- Roles divide into **ownership, control and compliance**; **business units own
  the model risk of their strategies**, and a named **model owner is accountable
  for performance, development, implementation, use and validation.**
- **Internal audit assesses whether the MRM framework works** — it **does not
  replicate validation.**
- **External resources can be engaged**, but need an agreed scope and a
  designated internal team overseeing them.
- **A firm-wide model inventory** — implemented, under development and retired —
  exists to **evaluate model risk in aggregate**, which is the only way to see
  risk that arises between models.
- **Vendor models still require validation.** Demand development evidence; where
  the vendor withholds internals, substitute **sensitivity analysis,
  benchmarking and stress testing**, and keep doing **ongoing monitoring and
  outcome analysis.**
