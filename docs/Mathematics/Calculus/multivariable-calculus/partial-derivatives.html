14.3 Partial Derivatives​

  • ∂f∂x\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}∂x∂f​ = rate of change in x-direction (holding y constant)
  • ∂f∂y\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}∂y∂f​ = rate of change in y-direction (holding x constant)

Geometric Meaning: ∂f∂x\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}∂x∂f​ is the slope of the curve formed by intersecting the surface with the plane y=by=by=b.

CALCULATION EXAMPLE:

f(x,y)=x2y+sin⁡(y)f(x,y) = x^2y + \sin(y)f(x,y)=x2y+sin(y) ∂f∂x=2xy(treat y as constant)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2xy \quad \text{(treat } y \text{ as constant)}∂x∂f​=2xy(treat y as constant) ∂f∂y=x2+cos⁡(y)(treat x as constant)\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = x^2 + \cos(y) \quad \text{(treat } x \text{ as constant)}∂y∂f​=x2+cos(y)(treat x as constant)

Higher partial derivatives:

fxx=∂2f∂x2,fyy=∂2f∂y2f_{xx} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}, \quad f_{yy} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}fxx​=∂x2∂2f​,fyy​=∂y2∂2f​ fxy=∂2f∂y∂x,fyx=∂2f∂x∂yf_{xy} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y \partial x}, \quad f_{yx} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y}fxy​=∂y∂x∂2f​,fyx​=∂x∂y∂2f​

Clairaut's Theorem: fxy=fyxf_{xy} = f_{yx}fxy​=fyx​ (if both are continuous)

14.6 Directional Derivatives and the Gradient​

Directional derivative in the direction of unit vector u=⟨a,b⟩\mathbf{u} = \langle a,b \rangleu=⟨a,b⟩:

Duf=∇f⋅u(dot product of gradient with unit vector)D_{\mathbf{u}}f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u} \quad \text{(dot product of gradient with unit vector)}Du​f=∇f⋅u(dot product of gradient with unit vector)

Gradient:

∇f=⟨∂f∂x,∂f∂y⟩\nabla f = \left\langle \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \right\rangle∇f=⟨∂x∂f​,∂y∂f​⟩

∇f\nabla f∇f ALWAYS points in the direction of STEEPEST ASCENT.

Properties of the Gradient:

  • Max rate of increase: ∣∇f∣|\nabla f|∣∇f∣ (occurs in direction of ∇f\nabla f∇f)
  • Min rate of increase: −∣∇f∣-|\nabla f|−∣∇f∣ (occurs in opposite direction)
  • Zero rate of change: occurs in any direction perpendicular to ∇f\nabla f∇f (along a level curve)

14.7 Optimization in Several Variables​

CRITICAL POINT: A point (a,b)(a,b)(a,b) where fx=0f_x = 0fx​=0 and fy=0f_y = 0fy​=0.

SECOND DERIVATIVE TEST: Let D=fxxfyy−(fxy)2D = f_{xx} f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2D=fxx​fyy​−(fxy​)2.

  • D>0,fxx>0  ⟹  D > 0, f_{xx} > 0 \impliesD>0,fxx​>0⟹ Local MINIMUM (bowl up)
  • D>0,fxx<0  ⟹  D > 0, f_{xx} < 0 \impliesD>0,fxx​<0⟹ Local MAXIMUM (bowl down)
  • D<0  ⟹  D < 0 \impliesD<0⟹ SADDLE POINT
  • D=0  ⟹  D = 0 \impliesD=0⟹ Inconclusive

14.8 Lagrange Multipliers​

Optimize f(x,y)f(x,y)f(x,y) SUBJECT TO constraint g(x,y)=kg(x,y) = kg(x,y)=k

METHOD: Solve the system of equations:

∇f=λ∇gandg(x,y)=k\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g \quad \text{and} \quad g(x,y) = k∇f=λ∇gandg(x,y)=k

Geometric interpretation: At the solution, the level curves of fff just barely touch the constraint curve g=kg=kg=k. At this point of tangency, their normal vectors (gradients) are parallel, meaning ∇f∥∇g\nabla f \parallel \nabla g∇f∥∇g. Therefore, ∇f=λ∇g\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g∇f=λ∇g, where λ\lambdaλ is the Lagrange multiplier.
