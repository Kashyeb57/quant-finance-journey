15.3 Polar Coordinates for Double Integrals​

Area element in polar: dA=r dr dθdA = r \, dr \, d\thetadA=rdrdθ

∬Rf(x,y) dA=∬f(rcos⁡θ,rsin⁡θ) r dr dθ\iint_R f(x,y) \, dA = \iint f(r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta) \, r \, dr \, d\theta∬R​f(x,y)dA=∬f(rcosθ,rsinθ)rdrdθ

Why rrr? Consider an annular ring at radius rrr. The circumference is 2πr2\pi r2πr, and thickness is drdrdr. The area of this tiny ring is (2πr)(dr)(2\pi r)(dr)(2πr)(dr), meaning the area element scales with rrr.

Polar Annulus
An annulus region defined by inner radius a and outer radius b. Useful for circles, rings, and sectors!

15.6–15.8 Triple Integrals and Coordinate Systems​

Triple integral over a volume EEE:

∭Ef(x,y,z) dV\iiint_E f(x,y,z) \, dV∭E​f(x,y,z)dV

CYLINDRICAL (r,θ,z)(r, \theta, z)(r,θ,z):

x=rcos⁡θ,y=rsin⁡θ,z=zx = r \cos\theta, \quad y = r \sin\theta, \quad z = zx=rcosθ,y=rsinθ,z=z dV=r dz dr dθdV = r \, dz \, dr \, d\thetadV=rdzdrdθ

(Useful for cylinders, cones, and rotationally symmetric objects around the z-axis).

SPHERICAL (ρ,ϕ,θ)(\rho, \phi, \theta)(ρ,ϕ,θ):

x=ρsin⁡ϕcos⁡θx = \rho \sin\phi \cos\thetax=ρsinϕcosθ y=ρsin⁡ϕsin⁡θy = \rho \sin\phi \sin\thetay=ρsinϕsinθ z=ρcos⁡ϕz = \rho \cos\phiz=ρcosϕ dV=ρ2sin⁡ϕ dρ dϕ dθdV = \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\thetadV=ρ2sinϕdρdϕdθ

15.9 Change of Variables (Jacobian)​

∬Rf(x,y) dA=∬Sf(x(u,v),y(u,v))⋅∣J∣ du dv\iint_R f(x,y) \, dA = \iint_S f(x(u,v), y(u,v)) \cdot |J| \, du \, dv∬R​f(x,y)dA=∬S​f(x(u,v),y(u,v))⋅∣J∣dudv

Jacobian Determinant:

J=∂(x,y)∂(u,v)=∣∂x∂u∂x∂v∂y∂u∂y∂v∣J = \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \begin{vmatrix} \frac{\partial x}{\partial u} & \frac{\partial x}{\partial v} \\ \frac{\partial y}{\partial u} & \frac{\partial y}{\partial v} \end{vmatrix}J=∂(u,v)∂(x,y)​=​∂u∂x​∂u∂y​​∂v∂x​∂v∂y​​​

∣J∣|J|∣J∣ is the "stretch factor" of the transformation.

Example: Polar coordinates x=rcos⁡θ,y=rsin⁡θx = r \cos\theta, \quad y = r \sin\thetax=rcosθ,y=rsinθ J=r  ⟹  dA=r dr dθJ = r \implies dA = r \, dr \, d\thetaJ=r⟹dA=rdrdθ
