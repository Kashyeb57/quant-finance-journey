2.5 Continuity​

A function f is continuous at a if all three hold:

  1. f(a)f(a)f(a) is defined
  2. lim⁡x→af(x)\lim_{x \to a} f(x)limx→a​f(x) exists
  3. lim⁡x→af(x)=f(a)\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)limx→a​f(x)=f(a)

Continuous: a smooth, unbroken curve — you can draw it without lifting your pen.

Continuous function

Discontinuities come in three flavours — removable (a single hole), jump (a step gap where left ≠ right), and infinite (the curve blows up at a vertical asymptote):

Infinite discontinuity — blows up at x = 0

Intermediate Value Theorem:

Intermediate Value Theorem

The derivative is the slope of the tangent line = instantaneous rate of change.

f′(a)=lim⁡h→0f(a+h)−f(a)h.f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}.f′(a)=h→0lim​hf(a+h)−f(a)​.

Visually, the secant through P(a,f(a))P\big(a, f(a)\big)P(a,f(a)) and Q(a+h,f(a+h))Q\big(a+h, f(a+h)\big)Q(a+h,f(a+h)) has slope Δf/h\Delta f / hΔf/h; as h→0h \to 0h→0, QQQ slides into PPP and the secant becomes the tangent.

Derivative — secant → tangent as h → 0

Three interpretations:

  1. Geometric: slope of tangent line to y=f(x)y=f(x)y=f(x) at x=ax=ax=a

  3. Kinematic: velocity if fff is a position function
