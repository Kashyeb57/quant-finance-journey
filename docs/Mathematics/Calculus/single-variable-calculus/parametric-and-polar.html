Chapter 10: Parametric Equations & Polar Coordinates

10.1–10.2 Parametric Curves​

Instead of y=f(x)y=f(x)y=f(x), use:

x=f(t)y=g(t)(t is the parameter, often time)\begin{aligned} x &= f(t) \\ y &= g(t) \end{aligned} \quad (t \text{ is the parameter, often time})xy​=f(t)=g(t)​(t is the parameter, often time)

Example: Circle x=cos⁡(t)x = \cos(t)x=cos(t), y=sin⁡(t)y = \sin(t)y=sin(t), 0≤t≤2π0 \le t \le 2\pi0≤t≤2π

10.3–10.4 Polar Coordinates​

Point P=(r,θ)P = (r, \theta)P=(r,θ) instead of (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) where:

  • r=r =r= distance from origin
  • θ=\theta =θ= angle from positive x-axis

Conversions:

x=rcos⁡(θ)r2=x2+y2y=rsin⁡(θ)tan⁡(θ)=yx\begin{aligned} x &= r \cos(\theta) &\quad r^2 &= x^2 + y^2 \\ y &= r \sin(\theta) &\quad \tan(\theta) &= \frac{y}{x} \end{aligned}xy​=rcos(θ)=rsin(θ)​r2tan(θ)​=x2+y2=xy​​

Common curves:

  • r=ar = ar=a → circle of radius a
  • r=acos⁡(θ)r = a\cos(\theta)r=acos(θ) → circle through origin
  • r=1+cos⁡(θ)r = 1+\cos(\theta)r=1+cos(θ) → cardioid (heart shape)
