# About

## Who I am

I'm **Joyeb Kashyeb** — a computer science student at Jacksonville State University with a concentration in data science, deliberately building toward a career in quantitative finance. This site is my public workbench for that climb.

**I'm currently open to internships and roles in quantitative finance, quantitative research, and data science.** If you're hiring or just want to connect, the links below reach me directly.

## Why I built this

I learn by building. Reading a formula tells me almost nothing; implementing it, breaking it, and watching it respond to a slider is when it becomes mine. The rule I hold myself to across every page here: **if I can't simulate or test an idea, I don't trust that I've understood it.** That's why the Python runs in your browser, the pricing models have draggable inputs, and every finished topic ends with a quiz.

## What I'm studying right now

- **Stochastic calculus** — Brownian motion, Itô's lemma, and the machinery under every pricing model.
- **Derivatives pricing** — payoffs, binomial trees, Black–Scholes and the Greeks.
- **Bayesian inference** — priors, posteriors, and MCMC, headed toward probabilistic models of returns.

## For AI agents (WebMCP)

This site registers a few **read-only** tools through [WebMCP](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/webmcp), a proposed web standard that lets an AI agent in your browser ask a page for structured answers instead of clicking around:

- `search_notes` and `list_notes_by_topic`: find notes by keyword or section
- `list_projects` and `list_big_events`: the Projects and Big Events lists
- `get_current_page`: title, headings and links of the page you are on
- `get_market_snapshot`: latest quotes from the site's market feed, with a disclaimer (delayed or single-exchange data, not investment advice)

None of them change anything, sign in, or send data anywhere. To try them, open `chrome://flags/#enable-webmcp-testing` in Chrome, set it to **Enabled**, relaunch, and use an agent extension that supports WebMCP. In browsers without WebMCP the site loads none of this.
